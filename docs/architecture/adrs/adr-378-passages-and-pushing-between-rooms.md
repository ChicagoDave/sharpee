## Context — measured, not assumed

**Exits are compass-keyed everywhere.** `RoomTrait.exits` is `Partial<Record<DirectionType, IExitInfo>>` (`packages/world-model/src/traits/room/roomTrait.ts:36`); `DirectionType` is the closed twelve-member union in `constants/directions.ts:8-21`; `RoomBehavior.getExit`, `setExit`, `isExitBlocked`, `getComputedExitDeclaration` and `resolveExit` all take a `DirectionType` (`roomBehavior.ts:27, 46, 62, 236, 271`); `WorldModel.connectRooms` infers the reverse through `DirectionOpposites` (`WorldModel.ts:1921`). `going` resolves its direction from `parsed.extras.direction` or a direction-named direct object (`going.ts:90-99`) and then reads the exit, the live blocked key, the door and the vehicle's admittance by that direction (`going.ts:319-415`). ## Decision

**D0 — Traversal is a system (ADR-379), and this record gives it its one home.** "May this mover take this way now?" is answered today in four places that disagree (ADR-379 Context: a vehicle on a heading drives through a blocked exit; an NPC walks through a closed door). This record adds one query to `packages/world-model/src/world/`, beside visibility and reach: given a room, an exit key and a mover — a walker, a steered vehicle, a pushed thing, an NPC — it returns the exit or the refusal, reading in order existence (D3's hidden), blocking (ADR-240's live key), the door (closed, locked), and admittance (D6: the way's `admits`, which names steered vehicles as ADR-366 D6 does and the class word `pushing`). `going`, the vehicle machine and pushing read it in this phase, each dropping its own copy of the gates; in the machine the one stepper that walks exits is `stepCompass` (`vehicleBehavior.ts:676`; the line, flight and open steppers follow stops, destinations and coordinates, not exits, and a streets vehicle is steered through `going`). NPC movement reads it in the follow-on ADR-379 D8 filed as GH #601. Sound's acoustic edges (`propagation.ts:161-198`) read the door through their own cost model and stay as they are; they are a candidate reader of the query, unscheduled, and are enumerated as an allowed reader until then. Deadly (ADR-227) stays a command transformer and computed exits (ADR-295) stay the resolver seam; both are consulted by the query where the mover is an actor, so no consumer re-implements them. A structural test enumerates the allowed readers of `RoomTrait.exits` and the `exit.*` keys and fails on any other (ADR-379 AC-4, the template).

**D1 — A passage is an exit named by its command: a line the room owns, in ADR-234's vocabulary.** A room block takes the line

```
passage <words> { or <words> } to <Room> [ through <door> ]
```

where each `<words>` is one or more literal words the player types (`xyzzy`; `climb the rope`), with no slot: a passage's words name the way, not a thing, so the `the` in `climb the rope` is a word the player types, not a noun to resolve. The first form is the passage's name; the exit's key is `passage:<first form, words hyphenated>` (`passage:climb-the-rope`). A form may not contain `to` or `or` (`parse.passage-words`). The line is led by the keyword because a room's body also holds prose, and a line beginning with an arbitrary word is a paragraph (`parser.ts:2600-2700` decides a line by its first word); every other room fact Chord adds is keyword-led (`exits:`, `admits:`, `aka`). **A passage is one-way by construction**: there is no opposite of `xyzzy`, so the loader infers no reverse, and `, one-way` on the line is `analysis.passage-one-way` with the fix-it to remove it. `through <door>` works as on any exit (ADR-234 D1), the door being consulted by `going` as it is for a compass exit. A passage with a body (the rope) does not get `through <thing>`: ADR-234 reserved `through` for doors and excluded the general cross-room primitive, and that stays. The rope is a thing in the room, described and examined as a thing; the passage's words name the way. The `exits:` line (ADR-362 D2) and the exit table (ADR-360 D3) stay compass-only: a passage is its own line, one per way, so neither production changes. `passage <words> is blocked [while <condition>]: <phrase>` and `passage <words> is deadly: <phrase>` take a passage as the blocked and deadly lines take a direction, naming a passage the room declares (`analysis.passage-unknown`). Two passages in one room with a form in common are `analysis.duplicate-exit` (ADR-360 D3a), as two exits in one direction are.

**D1a — The platform keys exits by `ExitKey`, not `DirectionType`.** `ExitKey = DirectionType | PassageKey`, `PassageKey` the template type `` `passage:${string}` ``, in `constants/directions.ts` beside `isDirection`. `RoomTrait.exits`, `blockedExits`, `RoomBehavior.getExit`/`setExit`/`isExitBlocked`/`getBlockedMessage`/`getComputedExitDeclaration`/`resolveExit`, `WorldModel.connectRooms` and the `exit.blocked.*`/`exit.message.*` keys (`going.ts:119-125`) take the key; `connectRooms` infers a reverse only for a `DirectionType`. `going`'s `resolveDirection` becomes `resolveWay`, reading `extras.direction` as an `ExitKey` (the parser leaves a non-compass value untouched, `english-parser.ts:473-477`), and every later read in validate and execute is by key with no second path. The vehicle's machine steps by `DirectionType` and never sees a passage. Nothing else in `packages/` reads `RoomTrait.exits` by a compass-typed key that would not accept the wider one (to be measured by `tsc` in Slice 1: the type change is the audit).

**D1b — `ExitTrait` and `ExitBehavior` retire (David, 2026-10-19: "ExitTrait is retired").** They are the stale vocabulary: an exit as an entity, unwired for sixteen months, carrying as fields the things this record gives the room's line (command and aliases → D1, D2; `visible` → D3; `conditional`/`conditionId` → D3's derived key; `blockedMessage` → the blocked line ADR-240 already has; `useMessage` → the room's own clauses; `bidirectional`/`reverseCommand` → nothing, a passage has no inferred reverse) and `listed`, which goes with the listing that was never built (D4). Blast radius, measured 2026-10-19: in world-model the three files under `src/traits/exit/`, the root barrel (`src/index.ts:55`), `behaviors/index.ts:12`, `all-traits.ts` (import, two lists, the union member, `isExitTrait`), `implementations.ts` (import, the `[TraitType.EXIT]` row, the export), `trait-types.ts` (`EXIT` and its category row), `entities/entity-types.ts` (`EXIT: 'exit'` and its `e` prefix; no story creates one), and the path-finder's exit branch (`WorldModel.ts:21, 1438-1439`), which keeps its door branch; in bootstrap the introspector's filter (`introspect.ts:84`) and its fixture (`introspect.test.ts:20, 65`); tests `world-model/tests/unit/traits/exit.test.ts` (39 cases, all the trait's, retired with it), one case in `entity-system-updates.test.ts:131-139`, and two stdlib fixtures that borrow `TraitType.EXIT` as an arbitrary trait type (`tests/unit/scope/sensory-extensions.test.ts:35-40`, `tests/integration/container-visibility-knowledge.test.ts:41-46`), which move to the inert test marker; docs: the `exit (e_)` entity-type line in `docs/core-concepts/README.md`, a retired note under ADR-346 D7, and the generated API reference on the next build. Nothing in Dungeo, UPPS, fernhill, Chord, story-loader, engine or world-index references it. `IFEvents.MOVED` and `IFEvents.CUSTOM_MESSAGE` lose their last world-model reader and stay; they are not this record's question.

**D2 — A passage's words are grammar only where the passage is.** The loader registers each form as a story-tier rule for `if.action.going` with the default semantics `direction: passage:<key>` (the shape the `directions` cross-product already uses, `loader.ts:1596-1610`) and a gate (D5) that is true while the actor's room — the containing room when aboard a vehicle, as `going` reads it (`going.ts:102`) — has that exit live (D3). A form's articles are optional for the player: the loader registers each form as written and with its `a`/`an`/`the` removed, mechanically, so `climb rope` reaches `climb the rope` and the author writes one form per real alternative. Outside the room, or while the passage is hidden, the words are not a rule: the input falls through to the standard grammar (`climb the rope` reaches `climbing`, `standard-en-us.story:517`) or to the parser's own not-understood, exactly as if the passage had not been written. That is ADR-370 D7's "parses as though the action did not exist", and the reason the global `define action` shape was wrong for a passage.

**D3 — An exit may exist only while a condition holds.** A single exit line, compass or passage, takes a `while <condition>` tail:

```
north to the Crawl while the boulder is rolled aside
passage follow the cables to the Sorting Room while the Cable Trench is not dark
```

While the condition is false the exit is not there, for every reader: `going` refuses as with no exit written (`no_exit_that_way`, or `no_exits` when the room has nothing else), a passage's words are not grammar (D2's gate reads the same answer), the vehicle's machine does not step through it, and the map places and draws it as a conditional exit. This is **hidden**, and it is distinct from `is blocked while` (ADR-240): a blocked exit exists and refuses with the author's phrase; a hidden one does not exist and refuses with the platform's. An exit that is both hidden and blocked is hidden: existence is read before blocking, so `going`'s hidden check precedes its blocked check (today blocking is read first, `going.ts:319`, because a blocked-only direction has no exit to read; a hidden exit's absence must win over that order or the author's phrase would print for a way that is not there). The inferred reverse of a two-way exit carries the same condition: a way is there or not from both ends, and an author who wants a one-sided reveal writes `, one-way` on each side. Not legal on an `exits:` line or in an exit table (`parse.exits-line-while`, the `, one-way` rule's shape). The loader lowers the condition to a derived evaluator `exit.hidden.<room>.<key>` bound at `bind()` beside `exit.blocked.*` (ADR-240 D2 Option A, `runtime/derived.ts:32-70`), and the one live read — `RoomBehavior.exitOf(world, room, key)`, returning the exit unless hidden — lives in world-model so `going`, the gate and the vehicle's machine (`vehicleBehavior.ts:672-687`) agree. The key names move to world-model with it; stdlib re-exports them for the story-loader's registrar. `PushableTrait.revealsPassage` — a message that says a passage was revealed and reveals nothing — is retired: the authored way is `while` on the exit plus a clause on the push (`on the player pushing … change the boulder to rolled aside`). `ExitTrait.visible` and its `conditional`/`conditionId` TODO were the entity vocabulary's unbuilt version of this same condition (D1b).

**D4 — There is no exits listing, so there is no `unlisted` exit.** "Unlisted" was `ExitTrait.listed`, the flag for a room-description exits list that was never built, whose other half is the dead `looking.exits` line (Context). Both retire together with the trait (D1b): the lang line goes, and row 64's "unlisted" is struck with a note that it named a platform flag, not a ruling of David's. An exits listing, if ever wanted, is a new platform default that changes Dungeo's rendered text and is its own record (plan standing rules); `unlisted` would be that record's word.

**D5 — A grammar rule may carry a gate; `define action <name> while <condition>` is its author surface (ADR-370 D7, built).** `GrammarRule` gains `when?: (context: GrammarContext) => boolean`; `findMatches` skips a rule whose `when` is false before `tryMatchRuleWithFailure`, counting it neither as a match nor as a failure for error reporting. The `GrammarBuilder` exposes it as `.when(predicate)` on the action builder, applying to every rule the block registers, as `.where()` applies to every rule carrying the slot (`loader.ts:1653-1659`). `define action releasing while the player is in On the Wire` compiles the condition and the loader attaches it to every rule the action registers, bare-verb prefixes included, so a shadowed standard verb reaches its own grammar and clarifications where the condition is false; `refuse when` is unchanged and still serves refusals inside the claim. A passage (D2) is the first user: its gate is "the actor's room has this exit live". The standard grammar registers no gates (the gate is a story-tier instrument; a standard rule that wants one is a design question, not a flag). EBNF: `define-action = "define" "action" WORD [ "while" condition ] NL action-line*`. GH #317 closes when this lands, with a comment naming D5; the Secret Letter interim it describes is that story's to remove when the port resumes.

**D6 — The thing says it can be pushed; the way says a pushed thing may pass (David, 2026-10-19: "Explicit > Implicit").** `pushable` stays one word on the thing, as today. Whether a push moves it between rooms is the connection's fact, written where ADR-366 D6 already writes who may arrive: `admits`, on a room (`admits: the car, pushing`) for all its ways, or on one exit line (`east to the Loading Dock admits pushing`) for that way; `admits` on an exit line is new, and takes the same list a room's does. The inferred reverse of a two-way exit carries the line's `admits` as it carries the connection. The per-object alternative — the thing listing its own pushing connections — was weighed and declined: it writes the map a second time on every pushable, the two-homes shape D1b retires; the exception (a boulder too wide for a doorway the trolley fits) is a clause the language has (`on the player pushing the boulder` with `refuse when the player is in …`). `pushable between rooms` is withdrawn; checklist rows 100 and 123 are answered by `pushable` plus `admits pushing`. The grammar gains `push the target <direction>` and `shove the target <direction>` beside the twelve `move` forms, which `lang-en-us` already promised. Semantics: the target is reachable, in the actor's room (not carried: `wearing_it`'s sibling `carrying_it`) and `pushable`; the way is the room's exit in that direction (compass only — a passage is not a direction, so `push cart xyzzy` is not grammar) and it admits pushing, else `cannot_push_that_way` ("{You} {can't} push {the target} that way."), a new pushing line. The actor's traversal is `going`'s, delegated (ADR-228 D6-B, `delegation-shared.ts`): pushing validates by delegating a `going` command for the direction, so every refusal `going` knows — no exit, hidden (D3), the blocked phrase, a closed or locked door, `vehicle_ride`/`vehicle_cannot_walk` when aboard (you push on foot) — is the push's refusal with the same wording, and every hook fires under both ids: `on the player pushing the cart` on the thing, `on the player going` and the destination's `after the player entering` as for any step (Context: the room clause is the went event's). Execute moves the thing to the destination first, then runs the delegated `going` execute, which moves the actor; report emits pushing's `pushed_direction` ("You push the cart north.") and then `going`'s events, so the new room is described with the thing in it. A direction on a pushable where the way does not admit pushing (`move lever north`; `push trolley up` onto a ladder) refuses `cannot_push_that_way`: the lie the Context measured ends, and a button pushed with a direction is not pressed. `push <thing>` with no direction is what `pushable` is today: the press, firing `on the player pushing` and the stock line.

**D6a — `PushableTrait` becomes a marker.** A pushable is one thing and the way decides the move (D6), so `pushType` has no reader and goes with its `heavy` and `moveable` values. The rest of the trait's fields were audited reader by reader (2026-10-19): `revealsPassage` is replaced by D3; `pushDirection` by the exit itself; `pushSound`, `requiresStrength` and `revealsPassage` are copied into the pushed event (`pushing-events.ts`) and rendered by nothing, so the event drops them too; `repeatable`, `pushCount`, `maxPushes`, `activates` and `effects` are read by nothing anywhere; `state` is read once, by Dungeo's rug interceptor as its fire-once guard (`rug-push-interceptor.ts:47, 73`), which moves onto the interceptor's own state in the same change — Dungeo is an outlier that must keep working, never a design input. What remains is the marker the language writes: `PushableTrait` with no fields, as `SceneryTrait` is. Dungeo's two constructions become `new PushableTrait()`; no transcript pushes with a direction, so the chain's text cannot move (AC-8). *(Was a [call] in the first draft; resolved by D6's shape.)*
