## Context

ADR-120 pins the after-action turn phases by priority: NPC (100) first — "their actions may trigger FSM transitions"; State Machine (75); Scheduler (50) last — "temporal events run last — daemons/fuses are background." ADR-328's amendment kept that relative order when the NPC plugin dissolved into the engine-owned actor phase (`packages/engine/src/actor-turn-plugin.ts`, priority 100). `PluginRegistry.getAll()` runs plugins by priority descending (`packages/plugins/src/plugin-registry.ts`).

That rationale was written before Chord. Under Chord, **every per-turn clause an author writes rides the scheduler**: `when <timer> expires` (ADR-325), `on every turn` (the `chord.entity-turn.*` daemons), `define sequence` steps. So the story's own reactions to the player's action run at 50, after the platform's character tick at 100 — and any state an author changes on the story's clock is read by the actor phase, the thread floor turns (ADR-320 D14), and NPC behaviours **one turn late**.

Measured (2026-09-02, session 6a3da1, the W-10 dance prototype `branch-stories/secret-letter/prototypes/w10-dance/`, driven through `./sharpee play branch-stories/secret-letter/prototypes/w10-dance` one command per 350 ms): the turn whose `wait` printed `HAND-OFF` (the `when hand expires` clause making the third partner `dancing`) printed no beat; the next `wait` printed `(W-10 marker — third partner, beat 1.)` — the `opens when the third partner is dancing` thread opened one turn after the state changed, every hand, across two full rounds. The story-side answers — holding beats on the state, measuring a two-turn hand as three named turns — are the story reaching around the platform's order, the seam `docs/core-concepts/README.md` names as the recurring failure: a capability the engine has with no natural way for the language to reach it.

This is not a bug in ADR-120; it is a decision whose reason no longer covers the case. The two-sided obligation (core-concepts, "Where the work is") puts the fix on the platform side, because that is where the seam gets simpler.

## Decision

- **D1 — The principle: what the author wrote happens first; the platform acts on the world the author left.** After the player's action, the story's declared reactions run, then the platform's autonomous phases run against the resulting state.
- **D2 — The order is three named bands (resolved from Q-1, interview of 2026-09-02 — David: "bands of priority within the platform makes sense").** Priority stays the mechanism; the band is what a plugin's author chooses, by name. `packages/plugins` exports the bands as named constants, and a plugin declares its band and its place within it. Across bands the order is the principle; within a band the order is local.

  **The contract**: `packages/plugins` exports `TURN_BANDS`, a frozen object with one entry per band — `storyReactions` (300–399), `platformPhases` (200–299), `watchers` (100–199) — each carrying its floor and ceiling, plus `bandOf(priority)` returning the band name or `undefined`. `TurnPlugin.priority` stays a number: a plugin declares its band by taking its priority from the band's constant (`TURN_BANDS.platformPhases.floor + 50`), so no field is added to the interface and the registry's sort is unchanged. The band test (Acceptance 4) pins the ranges and every shipped plugin's membership. A story's own TypeScript plugin may still register any number (ADR-120: "stories can adjust ordering"); the registry does not refuse an unbanded priority, and `bandOf` answering `undefined` is how tooling would show it.

  | Band | Range | Who | Why it runs here |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Story reactions | 300s | Acting-statement flush (`chord.acted-events`, ADR-329) 390; Scheduler 350 — every Chord timer, every-turn and sequence clause, TS daemons and fuses; hunger daemon (`grows N each turn`, ADR-263) 340 | what the author wrote happens first |
  | Platform phases | 200s | Actor phase 250, state machines 240, scene evaluation (ADR-149/186) 230 | the platform acts on the world the author left; their relative order is ADR-120's, unchanged |
  | Watchers | 100s | Scoring rank watcher (ADR-261) 120, hunger crossing watcher (ADR-263) 120, promotion narrator 115, hunger narrator 115, chapters (ADR-330) 110 | read the finished turn and announce — a rank crossed, a band entered, a chapter begun |

  **Corrected 2026-09-02 (Phase 2, session 6a3da1)**: the interview's seven-row table missed the four plugins the loader registers as object literals (the acting flush at 150, the hunger daemon at 30, the two narrators at 20); the eleven above are every turn plugin a live engine registers, verified by the placement test. Every order a plugin's own header depends on is preserved: the flush before the actor phase, the hunger daemon before its watcher and narrator, each narrator after its watcher, chapters after the scheduler.

  Every relative position other than the scheduler's is preserved: the actor phase, state machines and scene evaluation keep ADR-120's order among themselves and now run after the scheduler; the three extension plugins already sat below the scheduler (chapters' header: "after the scheduler has stepped its timers") and keep that dependency. Chord's `define machine` (ADR-215) lowers to the state-machine plugin and keeps its place: a machine reacts to what the turn did. Scene evaluation's old note ("before daemons/fuses") was a description, not a dependency; under D1 a scene whose begin condition is a timer state now sees it the same turn.
- **D3 — No Chord change.** An author's `when hand expires` / `on every turn` clause that changes an NPC's state is seen by that NPC's floor turn in the same turn. Nothing in a `.story` file names the order; nothing in it needs to.
- **D4 — What moves the other way, named.** A scheduler daemon that watched an NPC's act in the same turn now sees it the next. In Chord that path is `after <npc> <gerund>` (ADR-329), which reacts inline with the act and is unaffected. In TypeScript stories (Dungeo) a daemon may depend on it; the walkthrough chain is the gate, and per the standing rule a change to Dungeo's rendered text is its own decision, surfaced, never buried in this one. Dungeo is a regression gate here, not a design input.
- **D4a — Propagation arrival is the second inline path (2026-09-02, session 69a114; GH #353).** D4's `after <npc> <gerund>` does not cover a fact arriving by propagation: that is a tick sub-step, not an action, and a Chord `on every turn while <npc> knows <topic>` clause is the language's only way to react to it. The platform already promised that clause fires on the tick the fact lands (`arrivalNarratedTopics` — the tick stays silent for the topic because the story narrates it), and the band reorder moved the clause's daemon ahead of propagation, breaking the promise by a turn. The fix keeps D1 and the bands and restores the promise where it is made: `CharacterPhaseRegistry` carries a load-bound `ArrivalReaction` (the oracle's shape — authored wiring, no runtime state); `recordTransfer` queues each newly written arrival-narrated fact and the tick runs the reaction for each as its last sub-step, after scenes — same tick, but after the goals and scenes sub-steps have seen the world as it stood when the fact arrived, which is the order the scheduler gave those clauses before this ADR (David, 2026-09-02: "Fix it", on the walkthrough showing Burbage following Kemp when the reaction ran mid-tick); the loader binds it to the runtime, which runs the owner's every-turn clauses gated on that topic through the same body the scheduler daemon uses, so `, once` spent on arrival is spent for the daemon's next pass. Not an event chain — the engine records a plugin tick's events without running them through the event processor, so a chained handler never sees them. Building it exposed a second defect the gate caught: an every-turn clause's narration was sourced (ADR-328 D3) to the owner's place AFTER the body ran, so a body that moves its owner placed its line at the destination and the engine tagged it absent — the runtime now snapshots the owner's place before the body and sources to that ("the place it happened" is where the owner stood when the clause fired). Real-path test: `packages/story-loader/tests/adr-332-arrival-reaction.test.ts`.
- **D5 — ADR-120 §Plugin Priority Order is amended**, not superseded: the table becomes the three bands; the plugin interface, registry, and everything else in ADR-120 stand. The amendment note on ADR-120 was written 2026-09-02 (session 6a3da1) and points here; ADR-120's own Status line is untouched by this ADR. This ADR's Status flips DRAFT → ACCEPTED on David's word after `adr-review`, and the session that lands Phase 2 of the plan stamps it IMPLEMENTED with the Acceptance evidence inline.

## Consequences

- The W-10 prototype's hold gates and the three-named-turn measure become unnecessary; the hand-off phrase and the next partner's first beat land on the same turn once ADR-320 D10's interruption (GH #348) is built.
- `packages/plugins` gains the band constants (compatibility surface — a small new export on the `@sharpee/*` lockstep, whose version moves at publish, not per landing); the eleven plugins renumber to the table above (acting flush 390; scheduler 350; hunger daemon 340; actor 250; state machines 240; scene evaluation 230; rank and hunger watchers 120; the two narrators 115; chapters 110) and declare their band; every header comment that narrates the old order (state-machine, scene-evaluation, actor-turn, chapters) is rewritten to name the band. ADR-120's "priority is a convention, not enforced" becomes: a band is a name you pick.
- Every story combining scheduler daemons with actor-phase behaviour is in the blast radius. Acceptance below names the gates.
- Extension plugins registered through `registerPlugin` (ADR-260 D6) are placed in the watchers band; the loader's own story-clause plugins (the acting flush, the hunger daemon) declare the story-reactions band, its narrators the watchers band. A new plugin declares its band or the placement test refuses it.
