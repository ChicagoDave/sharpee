# ADR-404: The opening is a routine the engine runs, not a turn a head types

**Planning**: IMPLEMENTED

**Summary**: The engine runs a story's opening itself, once, after the story is loaded and outside the turn cycle, as a routine whose default is prologue, banner, look and which the author may alter; no head runs its own opening `look`, and the opening no longer counts as turn 1.

So under `--test`, `--chain` and `--exec` the first command is already turn 1, and it carries the banner. **Other sites this reaches**, found with `git grep -n -E "'boot'|\"boot\""` on 2026-11-18, leaving out tests and generated files:

- The `boot` card type: `packages/branch-tester/src/tree-document.ts:125` and `:941`, `tree-walker.ts:378` and `:822` (the walker's `return 'look'`), `derived-runner.ts:931`, `claims/claim-lock.ts:252` and `:267`, `scripts/make-story-artifacts.mjs:150`, `tools/ide/web/testing-surface/src/cards.ts:995` and `model.ts:444`, and the copy of that surface the Swift head carries (`tools/ide/SharpeeIDE/Resources/testing-surface/surface.js`).

- The `turn` channel (`packages/stdlib/src/channels/standard.ts:347`): `contentType: 'number'`, `emit: 'always'`, `produce: (ctx) => ctx.turn`. Decision 14's blank turn count changes what it sends before the first command.

**That `look` is a full turn.** `TURN_STAGES` (`packages/engine/src/turn/stages.ts`) includes `pluginTickStage` and `advanceTurnStage`, so timers, sequences and every each-turn mechanic have run once, and the counter has moved, before the player types anything. The banner and the prologue are printed by that turn too: they ride on it because nothing else can carry them.

**What it has cost.** These are the cases found while the Chord manual was run by naive readers:

- A Chord sequence step `at turn 50` answers the player's 49th command, and a step thirty turns later the 79th (naive run 10, chapter 19). Replayed 2026-11-18 on Sharpee 5.6.3: in `tutorials/relighting/chord-v4.1.0/chapter-19`, 81 lines of `wait` and then `/quit` were piped to `sharpee play .`, and the output was split on the echoed `> wait` lines. The text "The light has turned" was in the reply to command 49 and "Dusk comes down" in the reply to command 79.
- `use hunger` has its meter at 1 before the first command, which chapter 35 of the manual has to explain to the reader. Run 2026-11-18 on the repository's bundle with `stories/hunger-demo/hunger-demo.story` (`grows 3 each turn`, `peckish at 3`, `hungry at 6`): `printf 'wait\nwait\n' | node dist/cli/sharpee.js --play --story stories/hunger-demo/hunger-demo.story` printed "A hollow ache settles behind your ribs." (the rung at 3) under the opening room, before any command, and "Your stomach knots and will not let go." (the rung at 6) in reply to the first `wait`.
- The browser client's boot path records the opening look as the play feed's first turn "or every replayed turn number would be off by one" (`BrowserClient.ts`, the comment above `:688`). That comment gives ADR-305 D3 as its source, which is that record's "As built" entry "D2/D3 — the boot look is the lineage's first recorded turn" (`docs/architecture/adrs/adr-305-create-transcript-from-play.md:203`): "The browser client auto-runs `look` at every fresh boot; the headless harness does not." ADR-305 is superseded by ADR-307.

**The engine already has the mechanism.** A turn is a declared list of stages. `META_STAGES` is a second list, for SCORE, SAVE and the like, and it has neither the plugin tick nor the advance; that is how a meta command costs no turn. What is missing is a list, and an owner, for the opening.

**Chord has a neighbouring block that is not this one.** `before the game starts … end before` (`packages/chord/src/ast.ts:519`, ADR-327 D10) runs while the world is built, assigns the player role, and refuses `phrase` and `emit` because "the block runs before any turn exists to carry prose". The opening is the first moment that can carry prose.

## Decision

Stated by David, 2026-11-18. Where a point is the session's reading of his words and not his words, it says so.

1. **The opening is a routine the platform runs.** After the story is loaded, the engine runs an opening routine once. David: "something in the platform after the story is loaded runs an on-before-story-starts event and by default this only contains 'look', but the author is allowed to alter this routine".

2. **The prologue and the banner are steps of that routine.** David: "we might put the prologue and banner _in_ this routine". The default routine is then prologue, banner, look, in that order, which is the order they are printed in today.

3. **The opening is not a turn.** It runs outside the turn cycle: no plugin tick, no advance of the counter. It is a third stage list beside `TURN_STAGES` and `META_STAGES`, and it takes no undo snapshot and adds nothing to the command history. This began as the session's reading and David confirmed it on the review's fifth finding (2026-11-18: "yes to all three").

   **Who starts it** (David, 2026-11-18, on the second review's first finding: "a"). `start()` stays as it is: synchronous, and it runs no opening. The engine gains one asynchronous call that runs the routine and returns what `executeTurn` returns, and a head calls it when it has no save to restore. The engine owns the routine and the head says only that the game is new. This replaces the first draft's "run by the engine from `start()`", which could not hold: the browser client calls `start()` (`BrowserClient.ts:633`) before it looks for an autosave (`:648`), and `start()` returns `void` (`packages/engine/src/game-engine.ts:665`) while a look is asynchronous. The session's additions, not David's words: the call is named `runOpening()`; it throws when `start()` has not run, on a second call in one engine's life, and once a turn has run; and what it returns is the first look's result, empty when the block has no `do-first-look`, while the prologue and the banner reach a head only as the packets of decision 6.

   **The headless runner makes the call too** (David, 2026-11-18, on the third review's first finding: "a"). Under `--test`, `--chain` and `--exec` the transcript runner calls it once, before the first transcript's first command, and never again within a chain. Every head then opens a story the same way, and a transcript asserts on the opening through the `(opening)` row the runner already has (`packages/transcript-tester/src/runner.ts:446`).

4. **No head runs an opening `look`.** Every head's own opening `look` is removed, and a head shows what the engine's opening produced. The engine's routine is the only opening. This began as the session's reading of point 1 and David confirmed it on the review's fifth finding (2026-11-18: "yes to all three"). The call sites are listed in Context.

5. **The Chord block is `when the game starts … end when`** (David, 2026-11-18, interview Q-1: "a"). It stands beside `before the game starts … end before` and does not replace it: the start block still runs while the world is built and still refuses prose, and the new block is the first that can carry it. It joins the grammar's existing `when … end when` blocks (`when <timer> expires`, `when <thing> moves`) and uses the words `define chapters` already uses in `begins when the game starts`. A story with no such block gets the default routine.

6. **The block takes three special routines and nothing else: `show-prologue`, `show-banner` and `do-first-look`** (David, 2026-11-18). On the contents, interview Q-2: "let's just implement the when-end when block with just the banner, prologue, and look for now". On the names, interview Q-3: "we might need to make it clear these are special routines...so show-banner, show-prologue, do-first-look". The names are hyphenated so that they do not read as ordinary statements or as the player's `look`. The default routine, which a story with no block gets, is:

   ```chord
   when the game starts
     show-prologue
     show-banner
     do-first-look
   end when
   ```

   The routines may be put in another order. Other statements, and actions the player could type, are not admitted. The three names mean something only inside this block, and one written anywhere else is a compile error, so a story cannot print its title a second time in play. This began as the session's reading of "special routines" and David confirmed it on the review's fifth finding (2026-11-18: "yes to all three"). It can be widened later without breaking a story.

   **How the order reaches a client** (David, 2026-11-18, on the second review's second finding: "a"). The engine sends one channel packet after each routine, in the block's order, and each packet carries only that routine's channels: `prologue` for `show-prologue`, `banner` for `show-banner`, and the prose channels with `preferred-layout` for `do-first-look`. The look's packet also carries the status channels a turn's packet carries, `turn` among them (decision 14); that sentence is the session's, added when the `turn` ruling showed the first wording left them nowhere to ride. A client that appends what it receives prints the author's order with no change of its own. Registration order alone cannot carry it: `prologue` and `banner` are registered ahead of the prose channels (`packages/stdlib/src/channels/standard.ts:704-715`) and would always arrive first in a single packet. The opening is therefore up to three packets, and the `opening` card of decision 12 gathers them in the order they were sent.

7. **A playable prologue story is not part of this record.** The interview on Q-2 grew into a larger idea: an authored prologue story with its own world, a passing setup routine, then the main story. David set it aside: "I'm cool with this being a rathole for later". The idea, his answers in order and its open questions are in `docs/work/prologue-ideas/prologue-story-ideas.md`. Nothing in it is decided, and nothing in this record waits on it.

8. **With `use chapters`, the opening chapter's card is printed automatically, immediately before the first look** (David, 2026-11-18, interview Q-4: "a"). It is not a routine the author places. Wherever `do-first-look` stands in the block, the card comes just ahead of it, so ADR-330 D4's rule that the chapter title is announced before the room holds in every ordering. With the default order this is where the card is today, after the banner.

   **How it is printed** (David, 2026-11-18, on the review's first finding: "a"). The chapters extension announces a chapter from a turn plugin's `onAfterAction`, which the engine calls during the plugin tick (`packages/extensions/chapters/src/chapters-plugin.ts:123`), and decision 3 takes that tick out of the opening. So the plugin contract (`packages/plugins/src/turn-plugin.ts`) gains one optional member, `onOpening?(context: TurnPluginContext): ISemanticEvent[]`, which the engine calls once on every plugin that has it, just before `do-first-look` and outside any tick. The chapters plugin implements it and returns its opening card. No other plugin implements it, so nothing else moves. The hook is not called when the block leaves `do-first-look` out.

   **Without a first look the card waits for the first command** (David, 2026-11-18, on the second review's third finding: "a"). The plugin's `onAfterAction` announces any current chapter not yet marked announced (`chapters-plugin.ts:150-156`), so with no hook call the opening chapter's card prints ahead of the first command's prose, as ADR-330 D4 has it for every chapter. No new code is needed for that case. It follows, and is the session's statement: when `onOpening` does return the card it writes the same announced mark `onAfterAction` writes, so the first command does not print the card a second time.

9. **A block is exactly what the author wrote: any routine may be left out** (David, 2026-11-18, interview Q-5: "a"). Without `show-banner` the story never prints its title block. Without `show-prologue` the header's prologue text is not shown. Without `do-first-look` the first room is not described until the player looks, and it reads as a first visit then: a room is marked visited by a look (`packages/stdlib/src/actions/standard/looking/looking.ts:152`) and by arriving (`packages/stdlib/src/actions/standard/going/going.ts:512`), and the opening does neither. The engine adds nothing the block does not name, apart from the chapter card of decision 8, which goes with the first look and so is not in the opening when the look is not; it then comes with the first command (decision 8).

   **What `do-first-look` runs** (David, 2026-11-18, on the second review's fourth finding: "a"). It runs the real looking action, as the player, through the same execution entry a typed `look` takes. The room is marked visited, darkness and perception apply, and a story's `on` and `after … looking` clauses and its event handlers fire, all as they do today. Only the turn machinery is left out (decision 3), so the opening reads word for word as it does now.

   The session's statements, which follow from the above and are not David's words:
   - The opening's stage list for `do-first-look` is a turn's stages from `execute-command` onward (`packages/engine/src/turn/stages.ts`) without `plugin-tick`, `advance-turn` and `command-history`, and without the routing stages before it, `undo-snapshot` among them, because there is no typed input to route. The implementation plan checks each remaining stage against its module before it is kept.
   - A block without `show-banner` drops the pending `game.started` event. Today that event waits for the first rendered turn and prints the banner there (ADR-334 D1a, "As built": the turn path renders "with a `game.started` ahead of them on turn one"), so left alone it would print on the first command.
   - A block without `show-prologue` holds the `prologue` channel back, which otherwise sends `storyInfo.prologue` whenever it is set (`packages/stdlib/src/channels/standard.ts:494-504`).

10. **The routine is a list the engine runs, and a TypeScript story states it the same way a Chord story does** (David, 2026-11-18, interview Q-6: "a", which also answers Q-10). The `Story` interface (`packages/engine/src/install/story.ts`) gains one optional field holding the ordered list of routine names, for example `opening: ['show-prologue', 'show-banner', 'do-first-look']`. A Chord block compiles to the same list in the Story IR. A story that leaves the field out gets the default. It is not an event with handlers and not a method the story implements, so a TypeScript story can do here exactly what a Chord story can and no more.

11. **The routine runs only when a new game begins** (David, 2026-11-18, interview Q-7: "a"). That is a first load, a RESTART, and a restore that fails and falls back to a new game, where the browser client's `look` at `BrowserClient.ts:677` becomes the routine. A restored session shows what was saved and prints no opening. The head is the one that knows which case it is in, so it makes the call of decision 3 in the first three and does not make it in the fourth. A RESTART reaches it through the head's own boot path, because the engine does not rebuild in place: it stops with reason `restart` and the client reboots (`game-engine.ts:910`).

12. **A test tree records the opening as one `opening` card, and the `boot` card type goes away** (David, 2026-11-18, interview Q-8: "a"; and, while answering Q-4, "our testing system has to be adaptable"). The `opening` card holds whatever the routine printed, in the order the story's block gives, and its assertions are made against that. Every existing tree loses its `boot` card and is re-recorded, and chapter 38 of the Chord manual, which teaches the two cards, is rewritten.

13. **Nothing is owed to saves and recordings made before the change** (David, 2026-11-18, interview Q-9: "a - still greenfield"). An old save restores as it is, with the turn count it was saved with, one higher than the same position in a new game. No migration and no version check are written. The release note says so, and says that transcripts and test trees recorded earlier should be re-recorded. The repository's own are re-recorded as part of the work.

14. **The small rules** (David, 2026-11-18, on the review's sixth finding: "5 is a compiler error. 6 is it's blank, not 0. The others are fine").
   - **The list.** `Story` gains `opening?: readonly OpeningRoutine[]`, where `OpeningRoutine` is `'show-prologue' | 'show-banner' | 'do-first-look'`. The Story IR carries the same list under the same name, `opening`.
   - **A routine named twice** is a compile error in Chord, naming the repeated line. For a TypeScript story the engine refuses the story when it is installed and names the repeat.
   - **An unknown name** in a TypeScript list is stopped by the type. If one gets through, the engine refuses the story at install and names it.
   - **An empty block** is allowed and prints nothing.
   - **A second `when the game starts` block** in one story is a compile error, naming the second block. This is the session's rule, after `before the game starts`, which is exactly one per story (`packages/chord/src/ast.ts:519`); David said "yes" to it among the second review's corrections (2026-11-18), and the words are not his.
   - **`show-prologue` in a story whose header has no `prologue:`** is a compile error. The session's extension of that ruling to a TypeScript story, not David's words: the engine refuses the story at install, as it does for a repeat.
   - **The turn count before the first command is blank.** The status line shows no turn count until a command has cost a turn, and then shows 1. It does not show 0. Inside the engine the first command runs as turn 1, which is what `at turn 1` means.
   - **On the wire the `turn` channel sends 0, and a client shows 0 as blank** (David, 2026-11-18, on the second review's last open point: "b"). The channel stays a number sent on every packet that carries it (`packages/stdlib/src/channels/standard.ts:347-351`), and the rule that 0 is shown as no count lives in each client that shows a count. The browser client's status line is one (`BrowserClient.ts`, `renderCombinedStatus`, which prints `Turns: ${this.currentTurn}`); which other heads show a count was not established for this record, and the plan lists them. The session's statement, not David's words: the value is the number of commands that have cost a turn, so it is 0 through the opening and 1 after the first command, and the plan checks that against what `ctx.turn` holds today, since the engine's counter starts at 1 (`packages/engine/src/game-engine.ts:606`).
   - **UNDO as the first command** gets the answer it gets today when there is nothing to undo.

David, on the design as a whole: "this is one of those slap your head 'should have done this from the beginning' instead of all of the hacky mechanisms".

## Records this one amends

This record does not replace any other. It changes one statement in each of five, and each stays in force otherwise.

- **ADR-330 D4** says the chapter card lands "before that turn's prose and after the banner". Under decision 8 the opening card goes with the first look, so in a story whose block puts `show-banner` after `do-first-look` the card comes before the banner. For every later chapter D4 is unchanged.
- **ADR-307** says "the opening carries no command; the boot look is its own card". Under decision 12 there is one `opening` card and no `boot` card.
- **ADR-357** says "One card is one command, and that includes the boot card, which executes as `look`." Under decision 12 the walker runs no opening look.
- **ADR-334 D1a** is headed "Two lists, shared stages", and D2's order test pins both. Under decision 3 there is a third list, for the opening, and the order test pins it too.
- **ADR-298 D3** says `prologue:` "names the text emitted **before the banner**", and that "Placement before the banner is the platform's default client rendering order". Under decision 6 that order is the default routine's, and a story's block may put `show-banner` first.

**Who amends them, and when:** whoever flips this record from DRAFT to ACCEPTED adds a dated line to each of the five, at the statement quoted above, saying it is amended by ADR-404 and how, in the same commit as the flip. Their `**Planning**` lines do not change, because none of them is replaced.

## Acceptance

Each is checked on the built platform, not on a stand-in.

- **AC-1, the turn numbers.** In `tutorials/relighting/chord-v4.1.0/chapter-19`, a game of nothing but `wait` prints "The light has turned" in reply to the 50th command and "Dusk comes down" in reply to the 80th. Today they are the 49th and 79th (Context).
- **AC-2, nothing has run.** In `stories/hunger-demo/hunger-demo.story`, played with the command in Context, "A hollow ache settles behind your ribs." is not in the opening and is in the reply to the first `wait`, and "Your stomach knots and will not let go." is in the reply to the second. A timer started in `before the game starts` has not stepped when the first command is typed.
- **AC-3, the default.** A story with no block opens with its prologue, its banner and its first room, in that order, word for word as it does today. "Today" is a recording made before the change: the opening of one `test-stories/` story with a prologue, played in the bundle's `--play` mode and in the browser check, saved and compared after.
- **AC-4, the author's order.** A story whose block is `show-prologue`, `do-first-look`, `show-banner` prints the room before the banner. A story whose block leaves a routine out does not print that part.
- **AC-5, no head types the opening.** `git grep -n -E "execute(Turn|Command)\(['\"]look['\"]\)"` finds no opening look in any head listed in Context. That pattern cannot see the walker, so two more searches go with it: `git grep -n -E "'boot'|\"boot\""` finds no `boot` card type in any site listed under "Other sites this reaches", and `git grep -n "return 'look'" -- packages/branch-tester/src` finds nothing.
- **AC-6, the chapter card.** With `use chapters`, the opening card is printed immediately before the first room in the default order and in the order of AC-4, and is printed once, never twice. When the block has no `do-first-look` the opening has no card and the first command's reply opens with it. `packages/platform-browser/tests/chapter-before-room.test.ts` still passes.
- **AC-7, refusals.** The compiler refuses, each with its own message: a routine named twice; `show-prologue` in a story with no `prologue:`; any other statement inside the block; one of the three names outside the block; a second `when the game starts` block. The engine refuses at install a TypeScript story whose list repeats a routine, names an unknown one, or names `show-prologue` when the story has no prologue.

## As built

Built in the eight phases of `docs/work/opening-routine/plan.md`, 2026-11-18, and published as Sharpee 5.6.4 on 2026-11-19. The acceptance criteria were re-run together on that build, and all eleven hold: `docs/work/opening-routine/acceptance.md`. The per-phase record is `docs/work/opening-routine/census.md`, sections 9 to 14. The rulings below are David's, each made in the phase that reached it; where one departs from the Decision text, the code follows the ruling.

- **The opening's stage list has its own stage entries.** Six stages of the `do-first-look` list name, in `requires`, stages the opening omits. Each kept module's `run` is reused and its `requires` restated for that list (`inOpening`, `packages/engine/src/turn/stages.ts:106`). ADR-334 D2's check is unchanged, so this needed no ruling.
- **One packet per routine is built by a subset, not by stripping.** `ChannelService.build` takes an optional `only` list of channel ids and produces only those (`packages/channel-service/src/channel-service.ts:73`). Stripping a full packet in the engine does not hold, because `build` caches every channel's value as sent. This is the `packages/channel-service` change the Scope line now names.
- **The look's status channels.** The look's packet carries every channel a turn's packet carries except `prologue` and `banner`. A block with no `do-first-look` sends no status channels (`info`, `ifid`, `location`, `score`, `turn`, `prompt`) until the first command, so a browser page shows no title or status until then.
- **A meta command's `turn` value.** A meta command's packet is stamped `currentTurn - 1` (`packages/engine/src/turn/meta-render.ts:44`), so the `turn` channel says 0 when a meta command is typed first and 1 after one command. Before, SCORE after one command said 2.
- **What `do-first-look` enters departs from decision 9's words.** Decision 9 says "the same execution entry a typed `look` takes". As built, the routine enters the executor through `executeAsActor` with `if.action.looking` as the player (`packages/engine/src/turn/opening-look.ts:31`). It skips the parse, the parsed-command transformers and the command validator, and lands in the same `runPhases`, so a story whose grammar changes what `look` means does not change what the opening runs.
- **`start()` does not stay as it is.** Decision 3 says it does. As built, the routine writes the prologue at `show-prologue`, and `start()` no longer resolves it. The transitional write that kept Phases 2 to 5 byte-identical was removed in Phase 6.
- **The browser prints the prologue into the log in arrival order.** The client's `#sharpee-prologue` element and the layout's `prologue` slot are gone, so decision 6's "prints the author's order with no change of its own" holds for the browser too. ADR-298 D3 carries the dated amendment, and AC-3's browser recording was made again for that one difference.
- **world-index's start-block command kind is `start`, not `boot`.** `packages/branch-tester/src/derived-runner.ts` was listed here as a boot-card site and is not one: it reads world-index's start-block kind, the moment `before the game starts` runs. The kind was renamed (`packages/world-index/src/branches.ts:189`) so AC-5's search means what it says. The search's only remaining hits are the migration script's constant and its test's fixture.
- **The bundle's banner follows `sharpee play`'s line order**: title, subtitle, credits, story version, platform version. AC-3's command-line recording had two lines swapped for it.
- **The manual teaches the block.** The record did not ask for it; chapter 3 of the Chord manual has a section, The opening.

The workarounds named under Consequences, each with its verdict:

- **`turnOffset`: removed.** It was wrong at both restore seams, before this work and after it. A restore sets the engine's counter back from the save, so the `turn` channel goes on from the right number with nothing added (GH #800).
- **The play feed's `[SKIP]` first turn: nothing records one any more.** The feed's first record is the opening, marked as such, and the Testing surface seats the opening card from it and makes no turn card.
- **Chapter 35's sentence about the opening being a turn: rewritten**, with its hunger numbers measured again.

What the Consequences expected and did not happen:

- **The manual's locked claims did not move.** The claims locked "at turn 82" and "at turn 138" still hold there, because a lock counts the line's typed commands and the first look was never one. Chapter 39 is unedited.
- **No Dungeo line moved.** The census found no line that differs, so nothing was re-recorded (`docs/work/opening-routine/dungeo-census.md`).

Left open: GH #811 (a failed restore is a new game only when the engine refuses the save before touching the world), GH #812 (the browser's prologue move has had no mutation-verification review and no look in a real browser), GH #813 (non-gating recordings that fail before and after this work), GH #814 (a `.transcript` opening row has no prose to search), and GH #808 (the three recipe stories were not regenerated).
