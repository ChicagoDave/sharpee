# Jack Toresal and The Secret Letter — narrative spine and text slots

This is the whole story in order, as the remake plays it, with a numbered slot
at every place where text is still to be written or revised. Everything not in
a slot is Michael Gentry's 2009 text, already in the game.

## How to work in this document

The plain paragraphs are the spine: what happens, in order. They are a summary
for orientation, not game text, and need no editing.

Each numbered slot is one piece of game text. Write under **Your text** and
leave everything else as it is. Slots can be done in any order, and any slot
left as *(write here)* stays a placeholder in the game. A blank line in your
text becomes a paragraph break.

Each slot is one of three kinds:

- **NEW** — the remake added this moment; there is no 2009 text.
- **REVISE** — 2009 text exists but no longer fits. The original is shown.
- **CONVERSATION** — the slot stands in for one or more exchanges from the
  2009 game's conversation trees, which are being rewritten rather than
  carried. The slot says what the conversation has to do; the original
  exchanges are in the Appendix at the end, one section per speaker.

**In the game now** shows what plays today. Where it is labelled a placeholder,
it is a stand-in drafted during the port so the game can be played end to end.
It is not finished text and is marked in the source to be replaced. In
CONVERSATION slots the placeholder is a digest of Gentry's exchanges, squeezed
into one passage.

The last line of each slot says where the text lives in the source, for whoever
folds the writing back in.

## Ground rules that bear on every line

These are settled. The full statements are in
`docs/work/secret-letter-port/vision.md` and `change-document.md`.

- **Jack is a transgender girl.** The public reads her as a boy until the bath
  and the mirror at Red Gate (Chapter VIII), and as a young lady after it.
- **Some people see her as she is.** It is a talent, scattered at random, never
  explained. Those who see: Teisha, Dame Sandler, Widow Shannon, Olgan Minor
  (hostile), Olmer, Fossville's butler, Hester Rudup (hostile), the Prince of
  Gravesal, and children generally. Those who do not: the market and its
  stallkeepers, Germaise, Holstenoffer, Bobby, both Jacobses, Widow Theresa,
  Widow Fiona, Baron Fossville, Pieter. Fiona, Fossville and Pieter know who
  she is without seeing it.
- **Position picks the word.** People who see her and owe nobody anything use
  the true words freely (Teisha, Shannon). Dame Sandler uses them only in
  private. Those who know but do not see use the public words. Everyone else
  never speaks of her birth at all. Where a neutral word will carry a line,
  use one: child, urchin, kid, and above all **brat**.
- **The claim.** The old Duke was the King's brother, and the King died naming
  no heir. Jack is the heir, and no one is supposed to know. The Duke's hidden
  letter names her Jacqueline Toresal.
- **The mercenaries hunt a boy.** They know there is a scion, not who.
- **Fear without teeth.** Several new beats are marked "no way to fail": they
  frighten and cannot catch her. Where a slot says it ends the game, it does.
- **The Vedd.** The ending rests on a people the city speaks of only in worn
  figures of speech. That idiom is meant to season dialogue throughout, never
  to explain itself (vision.md §2 and §3h).

## Chapter I — Grubber's Market

The market is a ring of stalls around a centre post, and for now it is simply hers to wander. Nobody is after her. She can look at everything, lift things quietly, and talk to the stallkeepers. The way east to Commerce Street turns her back; she has no reason to leave.

## Chapter II — Commerce Street

She is out. On Commerce Street the dress and hat go back into the satchel and the cap goes back on: Jack the urchin again, among a calmer and richer class of merchant. Nobody chases her here. The only pressure in this chapter is her own nerves.

### 32 · Arriving on Commerce Street · REVISE

**When:** The first time Jack reaches Commerce Street, straight from the market.

**Original (Gentry, 2009):**

> You made it.
>
> You let out a long, shaky breath. You're safe now. Commerce Street is home to a slightly more upscale class of merchant, and business here is not quite as frantic as in Grubber's Market. Transactions tend to be calmer and more deliberate, and involve significantly more money. The Lord's Guard will be far less tolerant of a pack of armed bullies menacing the citizens of the upper class, so it's unlikely that the mercenaries will follow you here. And even if they did, there are too many streets and side alleys for them to block off your escape.
