# Chord manual: the game the chapters build

**Written**: 2026-11-13, session 625c8d. A design, not yet a line of Chord.
**Authority**: David, 2026-11-13: "you have authority over plot, story, puzzles, and prose", with
one premise: "a story that lines up after Spellbreaker when magic and spells work again."
**Chapter numbers** refer to `chapters.md` beside this file.

## How the premise is read

The premise is a world that lost its magic and is getting it back. The game takes that moment and
nothing else. It is an original world with its own places, people and spells: no names, characters,
spells, objects or places from Infocom's games appear in it, and it does not claim to be their
sequel. A reader who knows where that trilogy ended will recognise the morning after; a reader who
does not loses nothing. If David wants it tied closer than that, that is his call to make.

## The Relighting (working title)

**The world.** Thirty years ago the magic went out of the world in a single night, which people
call the Dimming. Lanternhall, a college of the old craft, sent its students home within the week
and was shut. Its porter stayed on to keep the roof whole and the doors locked. The spellbooks in
its library went blank that night and have stayed blank.

**This morning.** The pilot flame in the porter's lamp is burning blue. Ink is seeping back onto
blank pages, a word at a time. The magic is returning, weakly, and the old porter knows what the
college's founders wrote over the gate: the craft needs a light to come home to. The Great Lantern
on the roof has been dark since the Dimming. If it is burning by dusk, the magic holds. If it is
not, it gutters out again, perhaps for good.

**The player.** Ansel, who was a first-year student at Lanternhall when the Dimming came, and who
went home with one term of the craft and no use for it. Thirty years on, in another town and
another trade, Ansel felt something: a word half remembered, a candle that leaned toward a hand.
It was enough to walk back. Ansel arrives at the gate this morning knowing the Hall as it was,
remembering the shape of the workings but none of their words, and has a borrowed ring of keys, a
cold lamp, and one day.

**People.**
- **Hobb**, the porter, who was already old when Ansel was a student and has kept the Hall alone
  since. Too stiff for the stairs, full of half-remembered lore, and cautious about all of it. He
  knows Ansel's face before he can find the name. The game's source of hints and its first
  conversation.
- **Tamsin Vey**, a salvager who has been stripping brass from the closed wings for years and has
  her eye on the Lantern's lens. Not a villain: she has principles, a price, and good reasons to
  doubt that the magic is worth having back.
- **The wisp**, a scrap of returning magic that takes a liking to Ansel and follows the lamp.

**Spells.** The craft calls them workings. A working is a single spoken verb, and Ansel learns one
again by reading its page once the ink has come back. Each is a story action, and each thing that can
answer a working carries a story trait that says how.
- **kindle**: wakes a flame in anything made to hold one. The lamp, the refectory candles, the
  Lantern.
- **mend**: makes a broken thing whole. The cracked lens, the snapped bell rope.
- **hush**: quiets a thing that is making noise. The tower bell, the shrieking weathercock.
- **unbind**: releases a ward. The sealed stair door, the chained book.

**The place.** Eight rooms in the main game: the Porter's Lodge, the Gate Court, the Cloister, the
Refectory, the Library, the Undercroft (dark), the Bell Stair, and the Lantern Gallery on the roof.
Two regions, the Hall and the Grounds. A book hoist runs between the Undercroft, the Library and
the Gallery. Behind the Hall lies the Mere, open water with a beacon island, used only by the
epilogue.

**The through-line.**
1. In the Lodge, Ansel takes the lamp and opens the key press for the library key.
2. In the Library the kindle page has come back. Ansel reads it and learns the first working.
3. The Undercroft is dark. Kindling the lamp is the first spell the player casts.
4. The Lantern's lens is in the Undercroft, crated and cracked. The mend page is somewhere Tamsin
   has already been.
5. Waking the Hall wakes its wards. The tower bell begins to toll and will not stop, and the stair
   to the roof is sealed. Hush and unbind are earned by dealing with Hobb and with Tamsin, and each
   of them can be won round or worked around.
6. On the roof Ansel seats the mended lens and kindles the Lantern before dusk.
7. Epilogue, for the chapters that need open water: the flame is carried across the Mere to light
   the beacon.

**Endings.** The Lantern lit by dusk is the victory. Dusk falling on a dark Lantern is the quiet
defeat. Kindling the Lantern with the lens still cracked is the loud one. A rank ladder runs from
Apprentice to Lamplighter.

**Voice.** Second person, present tense, plain and a little dry, in short paragraphs. A sample of
the register, for the prologue:

> You were here for one term, thirty years ago, and you would have said you remembered none of it.
> Then last week a candle leaned toward your hand, and you started walking.
>
> The porter's lamp is burning blue. Hobb looks at it, and then at you, for a long time. He takes
> the key ring off its nail and holds it out. "Lantern wants lighting," he says. "By dusk. I'd go
> myself, but there are a great many stairs."

## How the game grows with the chapters

Each chapter that adds to the game ends with a complete, compiling snapshot of the story so far.
Chapters that teach something the game does not need yet (or ever) use a short standalone example
and say so.

## What the snapshots have shown so far

Part I and Part II compile at Chord 4.0.2 and were played with `sharpee play` on 2026-11-13.

- `light lamp` already lights the hand lamp through the standard action. For kindle to be the
  only way, the standard lighting grammar has to be taken away or refused for the lamp (chapter 25),
  or the lamp must not be a plain `light-source`. Decide when Part IV is written.
- Walking into the dark Undercroft prints the platform's stock darkness line, which names a grue.
  The Relighting overrides that message (chapter 22); it is not this world's creature.
- `sit on chair` is not understood; `enter chair` is. Chapter 7 teaches the command that works, and
  chapter 25 can add `sit`.
- Names must not share a word the player will type for something else: "key press" beside
  "library key" and two things called "lamp" each produced a "Which do you mean" question. The
  press is now the oak press and the sill lamp is the blue flame. Worth a paragraph in chapter 6.
- A person needs `proper`, or the room lists "a Hobb".
- A `rooms` group's description needs a blank line above it, like any description.
- The deadly parapet works; it was exercised in a scratch copy with the stair unsealed.

Part III compiles at Chord 4.0.2 and was played the same day. The Lantern's clauses and the two
lighting endings cannot be reached in the snapshot itself (no working can lift the grille or mend
the lens yet), so they were played in a scratch copy with the blocked line removed, once with the
lens cracked and once whole.

- There is no `if`. Statements after a `lose … when` still run, so a clause with two outcomes puts
  a `when` on every line, and a named condition (`lantern-ready`) keeps that readable. Chapter 16.
- A thing takes one `on` clause per gerund; two with different `while` gates are refused as
  duplicates, and the answer is one clause with `select on the <thing>'s state`. Chapter 17.
- A condition cannot say `the story is awake`; the bare word (`not awake`) works. Issue #705.
- A timer owned by a thing (`for the tower bell`) spoke its named turns only where the bell was.
  Owned by the story it is heard everywhere, and its `when … expires` clause goes in the header.
- `pullable` accepts one pull. A thing's own `on the player pulling` runs for the first pull only,
  and on a thing that is not `pullable` it never runs. Issue #706. The ordinal responses moved to
  examining the grille, where they work.
- `when the lens moves` did not fire when the lens was taken, dropped, or carried between rooms.
  Removed from the game. Read afterwards: `MOVE_TRIGGERS` in
  `packages/story-loader/src/event-contract.ts` is an actor's arrival in a room, a drift and a
  wash-up, so "moves" means a person or a floating thing changing place, not a thing being picked
  up or carried. Chapter 19 says so, and uses a person for its example.
- The score's maximum is the sum of the `score … worth` lines (30 here), and the rank is printed
  with an article ("the rank of a Latecomer"), so rank names must read well after "a".
- After an ending, `sharpee play` answers further commands with a raw engine error. Added to #704.
- Each candle is not yet its own thing, so `each` is not in the game yet. It arrives with kindle in
  Part IV, where one casting lights every unlit candle.

Part IV compiles at Chord 4.0.2 and was played on 2026-11-13 (session 5eae2b). The game can be won
from the committed snapshot: 44 commands, 50 points of 50, rank Lamplighter. The cracked-lens ending
was played from it too.

- Mend, hush and unbind are `define action` blocks, and `mendable`, `hushable` and `ward` are the
  traits that answer them. An action carries what must be true of the player (`the mend page must
  be learned`) and its miss (`otherwise refuse`); the trait carries what happens to the thing.
- Kindle is not a story action. `change it to lit` compiles and lights nothing: it stores a story
  state named `lit` that conditions then read in place of the real one. Issue #707. A story action
  therefore cannot light, open, lock, switch or un-break anything. Kindle is the standard
  `lighting` action with a verb added (`extend action lighting`), and its words are four
  `override message` blocks. This changes the growth table: chapter 25 gives kindle, and chapters
  23 and 24 are built on the other three workings.
- For the same reason the lens is no longer `breakable, starts broken`. It has its own states,
  `cracked` and `sound`, which `change` can write. Chapter 9 needs another breakable thing.
- A trait's `on the player lighting` silently switched off the own `on` and `after` clauses of
  every thing that composed it: the Lantern lit with no lens and no ending ran. Issue #708. There
  is no `wick` trait; the lamp, the candles and the Lantern each repeat one requirement line.
- A trait with one carrier is normal, not a smell: a thing may not answer a story action in its own
  `on` clause, so the grille's answer to unbind has to be a trait even though nothing else has it.
- `states: still, tolling, reversible` compiles, as three states. The form is `states, reversible:
  still, tolling`, and the runtime error's hint reads like the wrong one. Issue #709.
- The candles are one thing, not three, and `each` is still not in the game. Lighting several
  things in one casting needs `change the match to lit` to mean something (#707). When it does,
  the three candles and `each` come back.
- `sit on chair` works through `extend action entering`. Leaving the chair is `exit`; `out` and
  `stand` are not understood there. Chapter 7.
- `override message going-too-dark` replaces the grue line, which is what a dark room prints on
  arrival as well as on trying to leave it.
- A per-thing `phrase detail while the hand lamp is lit:` adds its sentence to the lamp's
  description when the lamp really is lit, and not when only the story state says so.
- A `define phrase <key>, cycling` with three variants serves all three workings' "you have not
  read that" refusal, and the cycle is shared across them.
- Provisional homes, until Part V gives them to people: the hush page is on a shelf in Stack 3, and
  the unbind page shows itself under a pricket when the candles are lit. The grille will not lift
  while the bell tolls, so hush comes before unbind.
- The walk spends about 44 of the day's 80 turns. Part V's conversations will need the dusk turn
  moved or the walk will not fit.

Part V compiles at Chord 4.0.2 and was played on 2026-11-13 and 14 (session d16329). The game can be
won from the committed snapshot: 44 commands, 50 points of 50, rank Lamplighter. The cracked-lens
ending was played from it too (the same walk without the mend).

- The pages have their people. Hobb keeps the hush page and hands it over when asked about the
  bell; Tamsin Vey carries the unbind page and gives it for a promise. The mend page stays in the
  cistern, so the growth table's "bargaining for the mend page" is a bargain for the unbind page.
  The candles keep their score and lose their page.
- Chapter 31's scene is in the game. When the waking timer expires the role moves to Hobb
  (`change the player to Hobb` in the header's `when waking expires`), who is `playable` and has
  his own `states: dozing, roused, settled`. Examining the newspapers as Hobb puts the hush page in
  his hand and moves the role back. Ansel is where the player left them, Hobb is listed in the
  Lodge again, and the score carries across. As Hobb, `inventory` is empty and the wisp stays with
  Ansel.
- There is no condition that asks who the player is. The newspapers' clause is gated on Hobb's
  state (`when Hobb is roused`), which is the working form and worth teaching as such.
- The dusk turn moved: the warning is at turn 70 and night at 110. The walk still takes 44.
- `follower` on a person with no settings follows the player from room to room and prints "The
  wisp follows you." The wisp starts with no placement and arrives by `move the wisp here` in the
  hand lamp's `after the player lighting`.
- A `goal` with `active when the tower bell is tolling` walked Tamsin from the Refectory to the
  Bell Stair one room a turn, then ran its `say` and `act` steps on the two turns after, each
  printed plainly when the player was in the room.
- The acting statement works in a topic row and an exchange row: `Hobb gives the hush page to
  Ansel when Hobb has the hush page` prints "Hobb gives the hush page to Ansel." after the phrase.
  `move the hush page to Hobb` puts a thing in a person's hands.
- Ansel needed `proper`; without it the giving line read "to the Ansel".
- Topic matching drops the article: `"the bell", "bell"` in one row is `analysis.duplicate-topic`.
  One spelling per topic, and both `ask hobb about bell` and `about the bell` reach it.
- `never` takes the third-person form (`never breaks a promise`); the grammar's comment lists the
  bare verbs. `feels wary of Tamsin Vey` and `feels trusts toward Ansel` both compile with a bare
  proper name.
- `it` is refused in a `define manner` row (`analysis.it-removed`); the row names the person.
- Manner beats never printed. They are carried on the event data and no play surface was found
  that reads them. Issue #710. Chapter 27 cannot show a manner block doing anything until then.
- A conversation runs one beat per ask of any of its `about` topics. Once its last beat has opened
  an exchange and the exchange is answered, the thread is concluded and its topics answer "I don't
  know anything about that". So a bargain that can be refused needs a second way in: Tamsin's
  topic table has `about "the price"`, gated on `the-bargain is concluded`, which opens the same
  exchange again with `then asks`.
- A `conclusion:` phrase printed as overheard speech (`You hear: "…"`), and `on parting:` printed
  two turns late in another room. Issue #711. The snapshot's conclusion is a `change mood` and it
  has no parting row.
- Inside an open exchange, an unmatched `say` prints the platform's "waits for an answer" line and
  the exchange stays open. `on silence:` did not fire on `wait`; when it does fire was not found.
- Greetings print on `talk to`. A person with no greetings block answers "doesn't respond".
- The inner-life and principle lines (`mood`, `feels`, `knows … witnessed, certain`, `burdened by`,
  `protects`, `temperament`, `never`, `answers honestly`, personality words) compile and load, and
  nothing in this game's play shows them acting. `change feeling toward Ansel to likes` runs in the
  exchange's yes row without complaint. Chapters 29 and 30 need a short standalone example whose
  outcome a reader can see, or a place in this game where a feeling gates a response.

Part VI compiles at Chord 4.0.2 and was played on 2026-11-14 (session d16329). The game can be won
from the snapshot: 57 commands and a `score`, 50 points of 50, rank Lamplighter. The cracked-lens ending
was played from it too. Chapter 35's systems (combat, hunger) are not in the game, as planned.

- The ending moved. Lighting the Lantern over a sound lens no longer wins: it makes the story
  `awake`, opens the water gate in the Undercroft, and says there is a second light. The win is
  kindling the beacon on the far side of the Mere. `relit` is worth 10 and the beacon's `answered`
  5, so the total is still 50. Night no longer falls once the Lantern is lit (the `lose … when not
  awake` line), so the crossing has no clock.
- The shutter is a `define machine` with one role (the crank) and three states. `when turning the
  crank: half` moves it, `on enter` bodies print and `change the shutter to drawn`, and the Lantern
  asks `the shutter must be drawn`. A machine's state is not readable from a condition; the machine
  writes a thing's state and clauses read that.
- A machine's `when turning the crank` fired on a turn the turning action refused ("isn't
  something you can turn", then the machine's prose). Issue #712. The crank has its own `on the
  player turning` clause, which is its answer to the verb and also where the third turn is refused.
- The book hoist is `a vehicle`, `movement: line`, `drive: state`, with three stops and a
  `position` block read after each turn: it is in the Gallery when it holds the lens and the grille
  is lifted, in the Library when it holds the lens, otherwise in the Undercroft. It moves the turn
  the lens goes in or comes out ("The book hoist pulls away.").
- Two `up is blocked while …` lines on one room compile and each speaks its own phrase: the grille,
  and `while Ansel has the lens` (the lens is too wide for the stair). The hoist is therefore the
  only way the lens goes up, and it will not go to the Gallery until the grille is lifted, so it is
  not a way round the ward.
- `put lens in lantern` takes the lens out of the hoist by itself ("first taking the lens").
- Aboard the hoist or the boat, `look` prints the room outside and lists "In the book hoist you
  see … Ansel". Worth one sentence in chapter 33.
- The Mere is `an expanse`, 8 by 6, `wind: from west`, with the Water Stair at 0, 2 and the Beacon
  Isle at 6, 2 as landmarks. Each landmark room needs `seen from afar:`. The Water Stair is reached
  by an ordinary exit from the Undercroft, `blocked while not awake`.
- The Heron is `movement: open`, `drive: player`, `commands: naval, compass`, with `sails: furled,
  full` and a `sailing` block. `enter heron`, `steer east`, `make sail`, then five waits: "The
  Heron arrives at the Beacon Isle." Mid-water, `look` names the expanse and gives both landmarks
  with a band and a bearing ("The Beacon Isle, in the distance, to the east.").
- `exit` on open water put Ansel out of the boat into the Mere without complaint. Not followed up.
- A vehicle's name: without `proper` the room lists "a Heron"; with it the article goes entirely
  ("You get into Heron") though the arrival line says "The Heron arrives". There was no way found
  to say "the Heron" throughout.
- The wisp follows to the Water Stair and not onto the water.
- `use expanses` and `use sailing` are header lines beside `use scoring` and `use chapters`. A
  fourth chapter, `crossing`, begins `when the story becomes awake`.

## Open points in the design

- Every mechanic above is a reading of the grammar. Each is proven, or changed, when its snapshot
  compiles and loads. Where Chord cannot say something the design wants, that is a finding for the
  language (issue #685's purpose), filed and taken to David, and the design waits.
- The epilogue exists because expanses and sailing need open water. If those two systems are
  better taught with a standalone example, the Mere goes and the game ends on the roof.
- Names (Lanternhall, Ansel, Hobb, Tamsin Vey, the workings) are first choices and cheap to change.
