## Key Decisions

### 1. The hand-back is a property of the fork and lives in its owner
ADR-307 D5's 2026-11-12 amendment already ruled that fork hooks are registered for the moment of a capture or restore and removed after. The misalignment was that the removal lived in branch-tester's `fork-save.ts` wrapper, so direct callers of `captureSave`/`restoreSave` (claims walk, outcome search) missed it. Moving it into `captureSave`/`restoreSave` gives every caller the same behaviour. Options A (route the walk through `fork-save.ts`), B (A plus stopping on a refused restore) and C (no source change) were set aside after David asked whether any was elegant and aligned with the platform.

### 2. No public save or load on the engine
Proposed, then withdrawn after David asked for encapsulation to be weighed. ADR-033 puts the boundary at the client hooks (engine serializes, client stores); `loadSaveData` (`packages/engine/src/game-engine.ts:1579`) swaps the event source, turn and history, re-derives the phase, and parks a reconciliation report the platform dispatcher collects. A public load would be a second door past all of that.

### 3. A dated note, not a new ADR
David said "Yes" to recording decision 1 as a note under ADR-307 D5's "An author's own save and restore cards" paragraph in `docs/architecture/adrs/adr-307-testing-tree-model-v2.md`.

### 4. A refused save or restore stops the claims walk with an error naming the node
Recommended by the main session to match how the derived runner stops a row, and approved by David ("Proceed"). Continuing after a refused restore would credit a command's result to the wrong path; treating an unsaved state as walked would empty the frontier early and let a negative claim stand unearned. The walk throws rather than returning a new stop reason, so the existing devkit catch turns it into exit 3.
