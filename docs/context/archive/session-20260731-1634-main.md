### 6. Investigation that grounded the new ADR
- **Dungeo combat read to source.** `resolveBlow` (`stories/dungeo/src/combat/melee.ts:177`) is a pure function; its randomness is one `int()` against a 9-slot table plus one `chance(0.25)`. Enumerated the MDL outcome tables in `melee-tables.ts`: KILLED appears only when defender strength is 1 or 2 — the five DEF3 tables (defender strength > 2) contain no KILLED at all, so no seed can produce a first-blow kill on a healthy player.
- **Worked the Troll case**: unarmed player at score 0 has `fightStrength` 2, troll `villainStrength` 2 → `DEF2_RES[1]`, nine slots, four distinct outcomes, no KILLED. Death arrives on blow two, two ways: UNCONSCIOUS → SITTING_DUCK (≈8.6%), or LIGHT_WOUND → `DEF1_RES[1]` where KILLED is 2 of 9 (≈7.4%). Depth-2 tree is 16 branches.
- **Found the detail that rules out a tables-only model**: `melee.ts:248` sets defender strength negative only `if (isHeroAttacking)`, so the auto-kill at `:197` is unreachable when the troll attacks — reading the tables alone gets this wrong.
- **RNG method census across all non-test source**: `.chance()` 22 sites, `.pick()` 13, `.int()` 8, `.shuffle()` 0, `.next()` **1**. So 43 of 44 draw sites already have a finite knowable domain — the fact that makes outcome enumeration feasible at all.
- **Dungeo systems survey**: ~25 gameplay draws across 14 systems. Thief is the largest (9 draws) and unbounded; melee is only 6. Four distinct defects surfaced: reachability (10 draws), partition (`int(1,100) <= p`, 3 draws), unbounded (`.next()`, 1), outcome-vs-presentation (2 message picks).
- **Verified `engine` → `stdlib` and not the reverse** (`packages/stdlib/package.json`), which forces the client to replay rather than search.
- **Found the existing author meta-command pattern**: `packages/parser-en-us/src/platform-grammar.ts:47` maps `trace` → `author.trace` with `stdlib/src/actions/author/trace.ts`. TEST needs no new concept, just the same shape.

### 7. Measured fork/restore cost (the Q-1 resolution)
Wrote and ran a benchmark against a real loaded dungeo world via `@sharpee/bootstrap` (scratchpad script, not committed):
- snapshot 436.4 KiB; `toJSON()` p50 0.91 ms; `loadJSON()` p50 0.69 ms; round trip **1.60 ms**
- flat under play — 15 real commands moved it 2 KiB
- **≈623 nodes/second**
- melee depth-2 search = 26 ms; thief 10-turn ≈ 27 min; thief 20-turn ≈ 51 years
- Conclusion: `FOUND` is cheap, `EXHAUSTED` is the expensive result, and the bottleneck is branching factor rather than serialization — a cheaper snapshot would be optimizing the wrong thing.
