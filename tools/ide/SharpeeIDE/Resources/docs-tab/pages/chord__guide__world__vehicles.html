A vehicle is a kind: a thing the player gets into or onto that moves them with it. Every vehicle block writes three lines, with no defaults: movement: names how it moves, carries: in or carries: on says where the player rides, and drive: says what moves it. A vehicle the player drives also writes commands:, naming the command set the player steers it with. Nothing about a vehicle is implied by its type.

The three drives: under drive: state the vehicle is wherever its position block says, and no statement moves it; under drive: control the story moves it with start, stop and send, bound to whatever the player operates; under drive: player the player's own commands move it, and commands: says which. A line or a flight starts at one of its stops or destinations. Moving a vehicle moves everything it holds.

A line driven by state

The bucket is wherever the world says it should be. Its position block is read after every turn, top to bottom, and the vehicle moves when the answer changes:

create the Well Bottom
  a room
  up to the Top of the Well

  The bottom of a dry well. A bucket hangs here on a rope.

create the Top of the Well
  a room

  The top of the well, open to the sky.

create the water
  a thing
  in the Well Bottom

  A pool of clear water.

create the bucket
  a vehicle
  in the Well Bottom
  movement: line
  carries: in
  drive: state
  stops: the Well Bottom, the Top of the Well
  direction: both
  holds 3

  A wooden bucket on a rope, big enough to stand in.

  position
    when the bucket holds the water: the Top of the Well
    otherwise: the Well Bottom
  end position
> enter bucket
You get into the bucket.

> take water
You take the water from the Well Bottom.

> put water in bucket
You put the water in the bucket.

The bucket arrives at the Top of the Well.

A line under control

The tram has a pace: and is started by the story. start <vehicle> puts it under way; it steps one stop every pace turns and stops by itself at the end of the line, where the next start runs it back. stop <vehicle> halts it where it is, and move <vehicle> to <stop> relocates it outright. The brake lever is the player's handle on the statement:

create the Depot
  a room

  A tram depot. The rails run north.

create the Midway Halt
  a room

  A halt halfway along the tram line.

create the Terminus
  a room

  The end of the tram line.

create the tram car
  a vehicle
  in the Depot
  movement: line
  carries: in
  drive: control
  stops: the Depot, the Midway Halt, the Terminus
  direction: both
  pace: 2 turns

  An open tram car with a brake lever.

create the brake
  a thing, pushable
  in the tram car
  aka lever, brake lever

  A brake lever.

  on the player pushing
    start the tram car when the tram car is not under way
    phrase brake-off
  end on

define phrase brake-off
  The brake comes off.
end phrase

define phrase tram-lurch
  The tram car lurches into motion.
end phrase

define sequence the tram ride
  when the tram car becomes under way
    phrase tram-lurch
end sequence
> push brake

> wait
Time passes...

<vehicle> is under way and <vehicle> is at <stop> are the conditions, and when <vehicle> becomes under way is an anchor a sequence can wait on. From inside a line vehicle, go north is refused: the player rides, and leaves with out or exit.

Streets: the player steers by GO

A streets vehicle moves along the current room's exit when the room on the other side admits it. Rooms, rooms groups and regions say which vehicles they admit with admits:, and it is the only place that says so: a room with no admits: line admits no vehicle, which is what a street map needs. The alley the car can take is the room with the line; the footpath is the room without it.

Compass: a heading, held until something stops it

A compass vehicle has a heading. Under drive: player, steer north and go north from inside are the same command and set it; heave to clears it. Every pace turns the vehicle moves one room in its heading while an exit that way exists and admits it; when it cannot, it stops and says so once. The story can also halt it with stop <vehicle>, which is how the anchor works here:

Flight: rooms aboard, and an exit to outside

A flight vehicle has destinations:, any to any. set course for <destination> (the destinations command set) puts it under way; it leaves its room, is nowhere for pace turns, and arrives. A vehicle of any moving type may have rooms aboard: aboard: lists them and boarded through: names the one the player enters by. Such a vehicle writes no carries: line — the player is in a room of the ship, not in the ship — and board and disembark work alongside enter and exit. Inside, an exit may lead to outside, and it resolves on every use to wherever the ship is now:

What the author never writes

A vehicle that moves over turns has a state machine built in — at a stop, under way, stopped — and the author never declares it, never names its states, and reads it only through is under way, is at, is heading and the becomes under way anchor. Its heading, destination and turns remaining are plain data on the vehicle, so a save taken mid-passage restores a ship that arrives on schedule. The author's own command words need no new syntax: extra words for a shipped set are an extend action on steering or setting-course; a vocabulary of the author's own is a define action with a directions block whose body drives a drive: control vehicle with start <vehicle> heading <direction>.

Seas and skies without rooms — a vessel on coordinates, bearings in degrees, wind and sails — are the use expanses and use sailing extensions: Expanses and Sailing.
