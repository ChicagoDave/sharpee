eat (eating): verbs eat/consume/devour X, plus munch and nibble [on] X, gated on the edible trait (not_edible). Liquids refuse is_drink ("You should drink that, not eat it") and solids refuse drinking with not_drinkable; there is no cross-routing. Food is implicitly taken first. A thing has one serving unless its block says servings <n>. Multi-serving food counts down (eaten_some, then eaten_all), and the last serving takes the thing out of the world: it is gone from the inventory, the room, and the parser's reach. The message honors the trait's data: taste (delicious/tasty/bland/awful), a poison effect (message-only: "It tastes strange…", no mechanical harm today), satisfiesHunger (filling / still_hungry). Event: eaten.

drink (drinking): verbs drink/sip/quaff/swallow/ imbibe X, plus drink from X and sip from X. Two things are drinkable. The first is an edible marked liquid: in a .story, the drinkable adjective, which composes the edible trait with the liquid flag set, order-independent with edible (ratchet G1). It follows the rule for food: one serving unless its block says servings <n>, and the last serving takes the drink out of the world. The second is a container of liquid (containsLiquid, TypeScript territory), open if openable. Container drinking decrements liquidAmount and reports from_container / empty_now, and the container is never removed, however empty. Verb flavor: sipped, quaffed. Event: drunk.

The transcript shows the cross-refusals, the implicit take, and the two ways a meal can end. The apple is the default: one serving, so eating it removes it, and the second eat the apple finds nothing to name. The cordial goes the same way: one drink finishes it, and the sip that follows finds nothing either. The biscuit is a story that wants something else. servings 2 means the first bite leaves it in hand, and its own on the player eating clause refuses the second, so the last serving is never eaten and the biscuit stays for the rest of the story, gnawed. Anything more elaborate is built the same way: a servings count for how long the thing lasts, and a clause on the thing for what each bite does.

One thing to plan for: once a thing has been eaten or drunk away it no longer exists, and a later rule that names it fails. Give a thing the story means to mention again a serving that is never taken, as the biscuit has.
