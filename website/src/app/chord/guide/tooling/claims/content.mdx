A claim is a test you write before the story: what the finished story can
reach, and what it can never reach. Claims live in a `.chord` file of their
own, named by your test tree's manifest, and the story never imports it, so
a claim never ships with the story. The claims runner proves each claim by
playing the story breadth-first through the real engine, trying every
command the claim's **necessary set** allows, and reports each one as
**held**, **violated** or **unproven**. A red claim is the point: the first
run of a story written claims-first is mostly red, and you write toward
green.

## The necessary set

Brute force over a whole story does not finish. A claim narrows the walk to
what is load-bearing on the way: the rooms it may enter, the things it may
touch, the verbs it may type. Nothing else is tried, so a claim is proved
*under its set* — a path that needs something outside the set is reported
as unproven, never as impossible. Declaring the set is cheap: it is the
walkthrough, read off as nouns and verbs.

```chord
claims
  needs rooms: the Iron Gates, the Gravel Drive, the Fountain Court, the Boiler Shed, the Entrance Hall, the Kitchen, the Greenhouse, the Folly Hill, the Folly
  needs things: Tobias, the stopcock, the primer plunger, the boiler, the garden shears, the sherry bottle, Mrs Kettle, the vine, the silver locket, the folly door, the fuse, the deed box, the deed
  needs verbs: ask, turn, push, turn on, take, give, prune, open, cut

  claim the story can be won
    the story ends in victory

  claim the deed is in hand
    the player has the deed

  claim Tobias has been asked about the folly
    Tobias was asked about "the folly"
    needs rooms: the Iron Gates, the Gravel Drive
    needs things: Tobias
    needs verbs: ask

  never the deed box leaves the Folly
    the deed box is not in the Folly
    needs rooms: the Iron Gates, the Gravel Drive, the Fountain Court, the Boiler Shed, the Greenhouse, the Folly Hill, the Folly
    needs things: Tobias, the stopcock, the primer plunger, the boiler, the folly door, the deed box
    needs verbs: ask, turn, push, turn on, open, take
end claims
```

The `needs` lines at the top are the shared set. A claim that needs less
declares its own `needs` lines: each key it names replaces the shared key,
and the rest is inherited. Claims with the same effective set share one
walk.

## What a claim can say

Each `claim` or `never` head is followed by one indented line stating what
holds. Where Chord already has a condition for it, that is the line:

- `the diary page is read` — a state the thing declares, or one of the
  platform's words: `open`/`closed`, `locked`/`unlocked`, `on`/`off`,
  `lit`/`unlit`, `worn`/`unworn`, `read`/`unread`.
- `the note is in the box`, `the deed box is not in the Folly` — where a
  thing is, or the room it has left.
- `the player is in the Study` — the room the player reaches.
- `the player has the deed` — what the player carries.
- `the story is midnight` — the story's own state.

Three forms only a claim can say:

- `the story ends in victory` or `the story ends in defeat`; `the story has
  ended` for any ending.
- `Tobias was asked about "the folly"` — a person was asked a topic, quoted
  as in the topic table.
- `the stopcock has been turned` — one of the thing's own `on` clauses has
  fired. The word is matched to the clauses the thing declares, so a thing
  with no `on turning` says so.

`never` heads the negative claim. It is violated by the first path that
satisfies it, with that path printed as the witness, and held only when the
walk has tried every state its set allows. A walk that stops at its budget
first leaves the claim unproven, never held.

## Selecting the file

The test tree's manifest, `<story-id>.tests/manifest.json`, names the
fragment relative to the story file:

```json
{
  "claims": "fernhill.claims.chord",
  "seed": 42,
  "story": "fernhill",
  "version": 3
}
```

Leave the key out and no claims run. Point it at a different file and the
run uses different claims, with the story untouched. A `claims` block met
inside a story, or in a fragment the story imports, is a compile error:
claims are selected, never imported.

## Reading the report

`sharpee test` runs the claims after the derived rule tests whenever the
manifest names a file, at the tree's seed, and prints the report after the
rooms ratio. A violated or unproven claim never changes the exit code; a
claims file that is missing or does not compile does, with the reason
named. Every run prints the tally first — how many claims held of how many
declared — then each walk's cost, then each claim with its witness or the
reason it stayed open. A positive claim held prints the first path that
reached it, which is the shortest under its set. A negative claim unproven
prints the budget that stopped the walk and how many things its set
carried: the states to exhaust double with every independent thing, so the
remedy is a tighter set, not a larger budget.
