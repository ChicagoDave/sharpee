A vehicle is a kind: a thing the player gets into or onto that moves
them with it. Every vehicle block writes three lines, with no defaults:
`movement:` names how it moves, `carries: in` or `carries: on` says
where the player rides, and `drive:` says what moves it. A vehicle the
player drives also writes `commands:`, naming the command set the player
steers it with. Nothing about a vehicle is implied by its type.

| `movement:` | What it is | `drive:` | The player's `commands:` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `none` | a bathtub, a crate the player sits in; it never moves | (none) | (none) |
| `line` | stops in order, both ways or one-way: a lift, a tram, a bucket on a rope | `state`, `control` | (none — the player rides) |
| `streets` | a car on a street map, moving along exits whose rooms admit it | `player` | `compass` |
| `compass` | a boat with a heading, moving one room every `pace:` turns | `player`, `control` | `compass` |
| `flight` | a ship that is nowhere for `pace:` turns and then arrives | `player`, `control` | `destinations` |
| `open` | a vessel on coordinates in an expanse — see [Expanses](/chord/guide/world/expanses) | `player`, `control` | `compass`, `coordinates` |

The three drives: under `drive: state` the vehicle is wherever its
`position` block says, and no statement moves it; under `drive: control`
the story moves it with `start`, `stop` and `send`, bound to whatever the
player operates; under `drive: player` the player's own commands move it,
and `commands:` says which. A line or a flight starts at one of its stops
or destinations. Moving a vehicle moves everything it holds.

## A line driven by state

The bucket is wherever the world says it should be. Its `position` block
is read after every turn, top to bottom, and the vehicle moves when the
answer changes:

```chord
create the Well Bottom
  a room
  up to the Top of the Well

  The bottom of a dry well. A bucket hangs here on a rope.

create the Top of the Well
  a room

  The top of the well, open to the sky.

create the water
  a thing
  in the Well Bottom

  A pool of clear water.

create the bucket
  a vehicle
  in the Well Bottom
  movement: line
  carries: in
  drive: state
  stops: the Well Bottom, the Top of the Well
  direction: both
  holds 3

  A wooden bucket on a rope, big enough to stand in.

  position
    when the bucket holds the water: the Top of the Well
    otherwise: the Well Bottom
  end position
```

```transcript
> enter bucket
You get into the bucket.

> take water
You take the water from the Well Bottom.

> put water in bucket
You put the water in the bucket.

The bucket arrives at the Top of the Well.
```

## A line under control

The tram has a `pace:` and is started by the story. `start <vehicle>` puts
it under way; it steps one stop every `pace` turns and stops by itself at
the end of the line, where the next `start` runs it back. `stop <vehicle>`
halts it where it is, and `move <vehicle> to <stop>` relocates it outright.
The brake lever is the player's handle on the statement:

```chord
create the Depot
  a room

  A tram depot. The rails run north.

create the Midway Halt
  a room

  A halt halfway along the tram line.

create the Terminus
  a room

  The end of the tram line.

create the tram car
  a vehicle
  in the Depot
  movement: line
  carries: in
  drive: control
  stops: the Depot, the Midway Halt, the Terminus
  direction: both
  pace: 2 turns

  An open tram car with a brake lever.

create the brake
  a thing, pushable
  in the tram car
  aka lever, brake lever

  A brake lever.

  on the player pushing
    start the tram car when the tram car is not under way
    phrase brake-off
  end on

define phrase brake-off
  The brake comes off.
end phrase

define phrase tram-lurch
  The tram car lurches into motion.
end phrase

define sequence the tram ride
  when the tram car becomes under way
    phrase tram-lurch
end sequence
```

```transcript
> push brake
The brake comes off.

The tram car lurches into motion.

> wait
Time passes...

The tram car arrives at the Midway Halt.

> wait
Time passes...

> wait
Time passes...

The tram car arrives at the Terminus.
```

`<vehicle> is under way` and `<vehicle> is at <stop>` are the conditions,
and `when <vehicle> becomes under way` is an anchor a sequence can wait on.
From inside a line vehicle, `go north` is refused: the player rides, and
leaves with `out` or `exit`.

## Streets: the player steers by GO

A `streets` vehicle moves along the current room's exit when the room on
the other side admits it. Rooms, `rooms` groups and regions say which
vehicles they admit with `admits:`, and it is the only place that says so:
a room with no `admits:` line admits no vehicle, which is what a street
map needs. The alley the car can take is the room with the line; the
footpath is the room without it.

```chord
create the High Street
  a region
  admits: the car

create the Garage
  a room
  in the High Street
  north to the Market
  east to the Alley
  west to the Footpath

  A garage opening onto the High Street.

create the Market
  a room
  in the High Street

  The market square at the top of the High Street.

create the Alley
  a room
  admits: the car

  A narrow alley, just wide enough for a car.

create the Footpath
  a room

  A footpath between hedges.

create the car
  a vehicle
  in the Garage
  movement: streets
  carries: in
  drive: player
  commands: compass

  A small car.
```

```transcript
> enter car
You get into the car.

> east
Alley
A narrow alley, just wide enough for a car.

> west
Garage
A garage opening onto the High Street.

> west
The car can't go that way.
```

The refusal names nothing: the Footpath's own prose says why a car does
not fit, not the platform's. Every vehicle refusal ships with a stock
line, overridable with `override message` like any stdlib message.

## Compass: a heading, held until something stops it

A `compass` vehicle has a heading. Under `drive: player`, `steer north` and
`go north` from inside are the same command and set it; `heave to` clears
it. Every `pace` turns the vehicle moves one room in its heading while an
exit that way exists and admits it; when it cannot, it stops and says so
once. The story can also halt it with `stop <vehicle>`, which is how the
anchor works here:

```chord
create the Open Water
  a region
  admits: the Marigold

create the Harbour
  a room
  in the Open Water
  north to the Sound

  A sheltered harbour.

create the Sound
  a room
  in the Open Water
  north to the Strait

  A wide sound.

create the Strait
  a room
  in the Open Water
  north to the Shoal

  A narrow strait.

create the Shoal
  a room

  A shoal, too shallow for a keel.

create the Marigold
  a vehicle
  in the Harbour
  movement: compass
  carries: in
  drive: player
  commands: compass
  pace: 2 turns

  A barque with a tiller and an anchor.

create the anchor
  a thing, pullable
  in the Marigold

  A heavy anchor on a chain.

  on the player pulling
    stop the Marigold
    phrase anchor-dropped
  end on

define phrase anchor-dropped
  The anchor goes over the side and the chain runs out.
end phrase
```

```transcript
> steer north
You steer the Marigold north.

> wait
Time passes...

The Marigold arrives at the Sound.

> wait
Time passes...

> wait
Time passes...

The Marigold arrives at the Strait.

> wait
Time passes...

> wait
Time passes...

The Marigold comes to a stop.
```

The Shoal is not in the Open Water region and has no `admits:` line of its
own, so the Marigold stops at the Strait. Under `drive: control` the story
sets the heading instead, with `start <vehicle> heading <direction>`.
`<vehicle> is heading <direction>` is the condition.

## Flight: rooms aboard, and an exit to outside

A `flight` vehicle has `destinations:`, any to any. `set course for <destination>`
(the `destinations` command set) puts it under way; it leaves its room,
is nowhere for `pace` turns, and arrives. A vehicle of any moving type
may have rooms aboard: `aboard:` lists them and `boarded through:` names
the one the player enters by. Such a vehicle writes no `carries:` line —
the player is in a room of the ship, not in the ship — and `board` and
`disembark` work alongside `enter` and `exit`. Inside, an exit may lead
`to outside`, and it resolves on every use to wherever the ship is now:

```chord
create the Pad
  a room

  A launch pad beside the harbour.

create the Orbit Station
  a room

  A station in orbit.

create the Sparrow
  a vehicle
  in the Pad
  movement: flight
  drive: player
  commands: destinations
  destinations: the Pad, the Orbit Station
  pace: 2 turns
  aboard: the Airlock, the Cabin
  boarded through: the Airlock

  A courier ship.

create the Airlock
  a room
  east to the Cabin
  west to outside

  The Sparrow's airlock.

create the Cabin
  a room
  west to the Airlock

  The Sparrow's cabin.
```

```transcript
> board the Sparrow
You board the Sparrow.

Airlock
The Sparrow's airlock.

> set course for the Orbit Station
You set course for the Orbit Station.

> west
The Sparrow is under way.

> wait
Time passes...

The Sparrow arrives at the Orbit Station.

> west
Orbit Station
A station in orbit.
```

Under `drive: control` the story sends it: `send <vehicle> to <destination>`.
`board` is for a vehicle with rooms aboard only; `board the bucket` refuses.

## What the author never writes

A vehicle that moves over turns has a state machine built in — at a
stop, under way, stopped — and the author never declares it, never names
its states, and reads it only through `is under way`, `is at`, `is heading`
and the `becomes under way` anchor. Its heading, destination and turns
remaining are plain data on the vehicle, so a save taken mid-passage
restores a ship that arrives on schedule. The author's own command words
need no new syntax: extra words for a shipped set are an
[`extend action`](/chord/guide/vocabulary/extend-action) on `steering` or
`setting-course`; a vocabulary of the author's own is a
[`define action`](/chord/guide/vocabulary/define-action) with a
`directions` block whose body drives a `drive: control` vehicle with
`start <vehicle> heading <direction>`.

Seas and skies without rooms — a vessel on coordinates, bearings in
degrees, wind and sails — are the `use expanses` and `use sailing`
extensions: [Expanses](/chord/guide/world/expanses) and
[Sailing](/chord/guide/world/sailing).
