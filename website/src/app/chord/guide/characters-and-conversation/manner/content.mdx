A **manner** says how a person delivers lines in a given mood, without
touching what the lines say. `define manner for <person>` holds one or
more `when it is <mood>:` blocks, and each block carries either `beat`
rows, a `voice` word, or both.

A `beat` is a small piece of physical business printed alongside the
reply — a gesture, a glance, a bit of stage direction. Several beats in a
block rotate rather than repeat, so a character with three beats does not
do the same thing twice in a row. A `voice` word colors the delivery
instead of adding business, which is usually what you want for a mood
that should make someone quieter rather than busier.

Manner is the cheapest way to make one authored line read differently
across a scene, because the line itself is written once. Reach for it
before writing mood-conditioned duplicates of the same reply: if the
words are the same and only the delivery changes, that is a manner, and
splitting it into two rows will drift as soon as one of them is edited.

```chord
define manner for Will Kemp
  when it is cheerful:
    beat "He sketches half a jig step on the spot, done before it began."
    beat "He snaps his fingers at a passing thought and grins."
  when it is stung:
    voice flat
end manner
```
