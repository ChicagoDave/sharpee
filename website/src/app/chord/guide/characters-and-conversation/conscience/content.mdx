**Conscience** is what happens when a person holds something that costs
them to hold. `burdened by <topic>` says a known topic weighs on them,
and the topic must actually be held: without a matching `knows <topic>,
<source>` line in the same block the compiler rejects it with
`analysis.burdened-unheld`, since being burdened by something you do not
know is an authoring slip rather than a subtlety.

Pressure accumulates into three bands — `clear`, `burdened`, `breaking`.
Bands are read like any other state: rows can condition on `when <the person>
is breaking`, a
[goal](/chord/guide/characters-and-conversation/goals) can go `active
when <the person> is breaking`, and a
[manner](/chord/guide/characters-and-conversation/manner) can shift the
delivery once someone is carrying too much. The band is not a countdown
the author has to drive by hand; it is derived from what the person is
holding and what has been asked of them.

This is where the rest of the model pays off. A principle
(`never betrays a confidence`) refuses to bend, a temperament decides
which way an unrelated conflict falls, and the cost of keeping both shows
up here — as a band that eventually makes a `breaking` row reachable. If
you want a character who cracks, do not write the crack as a script;
give them something to carry and a row that only fires once they are
carrying too much.

```chord
create Viola Wainright
  a person, proper, remorseful
  temperament desire over fear
  knows half-sister, witnessed, certain
  burdened by half-sister
  states: denying, confessed

define topics for Viola Wainright
  about "the family":
    change Viola Wainright to confessed when Viola Wainright is breaking
    phrase viola-deflects
end topics
```
