**Initiative** is a person speaking without being spoken to. `define
initiative for <person>` lists occasions, and each occasion says when the
character might take the floor: `on an open floor:` when nobody is
saying anything in particular, `on silence:` when the player declines to
speak, `when the subject changes:` when the talk turns, and `on <act>:`
for a witnessed act such as `on harm:`.

Every occasion takes an optional refinement, `, when <condition>` — so
`on an open floor, when it is cheerful:` fires only in that mood. Whether
a character actually seizes an occasion also reads their disposition, so
two people with identical rows behave differently in the same room, and
the same person behaves differently once their mood has moved.

Bodies use the conversation statements. `hold their tongue` is the
explicit choice to say nothing, which is how you stop a normally talkative
character from filling every silence. `deflect to "<topic>"` changes the
subject to one of the owner's own topic rows. `phrase <key>` speaks a
line, and `then asks <exchange>` opens an
[exchange](/chord/guide/characters-and-conversation/exchanges).

Use initiative sparingly. A character who takes every open floor becomes
the scene, and an authored `on silence` row in particular keeps a
conversation alive that would otherwise have ended.

```chord
define initiative for Will Kemp
  on an open floor, when it is cheerful:
    phrase kemp-interjects
  on an open floor, when it is stung:
    hold their tongue
  when the subject changes:
    deflect to "the offer"
  on harm:
    phrase kemp-on-violence
end initiative
```
