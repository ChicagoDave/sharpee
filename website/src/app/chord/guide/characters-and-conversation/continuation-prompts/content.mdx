A [thread](/chord/guide/characters-and-conversation/conversation-threads)
advances on the character's own turns, but the player can also ask for
the next beat directly. Four inputs do it: **tell me more**, **continue**,
**go on**, and **and?** — the frozen set, all equivalent.

These need no authoring. Any story with a thread gets them, and they
resolve their target implicitly: the prompt finds the co-located person
whose active thread has a beat ready, so the player does not have to name
who they are prompting. Nothing is guessed beyond that — if no thread is
ready, the prompt is inert and falls to the ordinary "talk to whom?"
response rather than inventing a reply.

A prompt is refused in exactly the cases a beat is not available: before
the thread's `opens when` holds, while a beat's own `when` gate is
waiting, while an [exchange](/chord/guide/characters-and-conversation/exchanges)
holds the floor, and after the conclusion has fired. That last one matters
— a concluded thread never re-claims its topics, so pressing "go on" at a
settled matter does not restart it.

Worth knowing when authoring: because the player can pull beats through
at their own pace, a thread's beats should each stand on their own as a
reply. Write them as things the character would say next, not as
fragments that only parse in sequence.

```
> ask shakespeare about the book
The pen stops. "The book stays in this room."

> tell me more
He watches what you look at.

> go on
"You always come back to it."
```
