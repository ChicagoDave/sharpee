A person can carry a **character model** — who they are — and a
**conversation system** — how that shows up when someone talks to them.
The two are one mechanism read at two moments: the character model holds
state (personality, temperament, mood, what they feel, what they know,
what they want), and every conversation construct selects its response
from that state. Nothing here generates dialogue; the author writes every
line, and the model only decides which authored line fits the moment.

Both layers are optional and additive. A person with none of these is
still a person, and a story that declares nothing compiles exactly as it
did before. Add only the layer a scene actually needs: a shopkeeper may
want nothing but a topic table, while a suspect under pressure may want
temperament, conscience, and a thread that will not let a subject drop.

The vocabulary is closed. Personality words, moods, dispositions, forces,
and the rest come from a frozen list, and a word outside it is a compile
error rather than a silent no-op — so a typo is caught at build time, not
discovered in play. Where a story genuinely needs a new word, it declares
one (`define personality`, `define mood`, `define temperament`).

create-line constructs that name a person (`proper`, `aka`, `pronouns`),
and [Behavior > Topic tables](/chord/guide/behavior/topic-tables) for
`define topics`, the plain ASK/TELL table these layers build on.

```chord
create Jonas Reed
  a person, proper, very impulsive, slightly honest
  temperament duty over fear
  never betrays a confidence
  mood anxious
  feels devoted to Marta Kell
  knows the-ledger-burning, witnessed, certain
```
