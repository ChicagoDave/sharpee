18 Counting, scoring and ending

A state says which of a few named conditions a thing is in. Some facts are not like that. How many times the rope has been pulled, how many workings Ansel has back, how well the player is doing: these are numbers. This chapter covers the numbers a story keeps, the score built on them, and the statements that bring a story to its end.

18.1 A counter on a thing

A counter is a whole number with a name. A thing gets one with a line in its block.

create the bell rope
  aka rope
  a thing, scenery, pullable
  in the Bell Stair
  counter pulls

  A rope as thick as your wrist, coming down out of the dark.

  after the player pulling
    raise the bell rope's pulls by 1
    phrase rope-sore when the bell rope's pulls is more than 3
    select cycling
      phrase rope-nothing
    or
      phrase rope-dust
    or
      phrase rope-patience
    end select
  end after

The line counter pulls gives the rope a counter that starts at nought. From inside a clause it is called the bell rope's pulls, the owner and then the name. raise adds to it and lower takes away.

A counter is compared with a number in words or in symbols. Both lines below mean the same.

    phrase rope-sore when the bell rope's pulls is more than 3
    phrase rope-sore when the bell rope's pulls > 3

The word forms are is at least, is more than, is at most and is less than. The symbols are >=, >, <= and <.

> pull rope
You pull the bell rope.

Your palms have begun to object.

Nothing answers. The bell has not been rung in thirty years and sees no reason to start for you.

That is the fourth pull. The counted blocks of the last chapter could have done this much. A counter earns its place when the number is read somewhere else, or goes down as well as up, or has limits.

The line may give a starting value and limits.

  counter sips starts 3 between 0 and 3

A counter with limits stops at them. Lowering sips when it is already at nought leaves it at nought. And set puts a counter at a value outright: set the vial's sips to 3.

18.2 A counter for the story

A number that belongs to no one thing is declared at the top level.

define counter workings

This one counts the workings Ansel has learned. It has no owner, so it is called by its bare name, and each page raises it in the arm where the page is first read.

      when legible
        change the hush page to learned
        raise workings by 1
        phrase hush-learned

The grimoire reads it.

  after the player examining
    phrase grimoire-waiting when any faded-page
    phrase grimoire-filling when no faded-page
    phrase grimoire-half when workings is at least 2
  end after

Chapter 16 said a condition cannot count. This is the way round it. The story counts as it goes, and the condition reads the count.

18.3 Scoring

A score is a number the player is shown. Chord builds it from scores, which are named awards with a value, each declared on the thing that earns it. The story header first says that the story keeps score at all.

story
  title: The Relighting
  id: relighting
  story-version: 0.3.0
  states: asleep, waking, awake
  use scoring
    rank "Latecomer" at 0
    rank "Second-Year" at 10
    rank "Lamplighter" at 25

use scoring turns the score on. It is the first use line in this book. A use line switches on a part of the language that not every story wants, and chapter 35 goes through the others.

An award is declared with a score line and paid with award.

create the spark page
  aka first page, leaf
  a thing, readable
  on the reading desk
  states: legible, learned
  score first-working worth 5
      when legible
        change the spark page to learned
        change the story to waking
        raise workings by 1
        award first-working
        phrase spark-learned

An award is paid once. If the award line runs again, nothing is added. The candles get score supper-lit worth 5 and the Great Lantern gets score relit worth 15, each with an award line in the clause that earns it.

The player asks with score.

> score
You have scored 5 out of 25, earning you the rank of a Latecomer.

You did not write the 25. The maximum is the sum of every score line in the story, and it changes when you add one.

18.4 Ranks

The lines under use scoring are the ranks: a name in quotes, and the number of points at which the player reaches it. The rank is printed after the word “a”, so choose names that read well there.

When the player crosses into a new rank, the story says so.

> light candles
You light the candles.

The flame runs down the length of the table, and every cold wick takes it. In the candlelight there is a leaf of paper under the middle pricket that the daylight did not show.

You have risen to the rank of a Second-Year.

A rank’s number is a count of points and not a share of the maximum, which has a consequence. The compiler will not accept a rank that no player could reach.

relighting.story:19:5 error [analysis.rank-above-max] Rank "Journeyman" sits at 30, above the 25 points this story declares — no player could reach it.

The Relighting will end with four ranks and fifty points. For now it has twenty-five points, so it has three ranks, and the ladder will be lengthened as later chapters add awards.

18.5 Winning and losing

Two statements end the story.

win lantern-lit
lose lens-shatters

Each prints the phrase it names and then stops the game. win ends it in victory and lose in defeat.

Both endings of The Relighting belong to the Great Lantern. Lighting it over a sound lens is the victory. Lighting it over the cracked lens is the loud defeat. The named condition from chapter 16 keeps the clause readable.

create the Great Lantern
  aka lantern
  a container, scenery, light-source, starts unlit
  in the Lantern Gallery
  holds 1

  A drum of brass and glass the height of a door, with a seat inside it
  for a lens and a wick as thick as your arm. Nothing has burned in it
  for thirty years.

  score relit worth 15

  on the player lighting
    the spark page must be learned: no-such-word
    the lens must be in the Great Lantern: lantern-empty
  end on

  after the player lighting
    lose lens-shatters when not lantern-ready
    change the story to awake when lantern-ready
    award relit when lantern-ready
    win lantern-lit when lantern-ready
  end after

The score line stands after the description here, and among the short facts on the spark page. Either place will do.

Every line of the after clause carries a when, and that is not untidy writing. A win or a lose does not stop the clause it is in. The lines after it still run. If the award line had no condition, a player who had just shattered the lens would be paid fifteen points for it. So in a clause that can end the story two ways, each line says which ending it belongs to.

18.6 Trying the endings

There is a difficulty. The Lantern is on the roof, the grille is across the stair, and nothing in the story can lift the grille until chapter 24. No player can reach either ending yet.

The start block trick from chapter 13 is made for this. Take a copy of the file as this chapter leaves it, which is the one “Build and try it” below copies in, since the endings are written there. In the copy, set the world up as it will be late in the game. Keep the copy, still named relighting.story, in a new folder beside your project called relighting-endings. It needs a folder of its own, since a project holds one story. Check and play it there.

before the game starts
  change the player to Ansel
  change the iron grille to lifted
  change the spark page to learned
  move the lens to the Lantern Gallery
end before

Then walk up to the Lantern Gallery, put the lens in the Lantern and light it.

> put lens in lantern
(first taking the lens)

Taken.

You put the lens in the Great Lantern.

The lens drops into its seat with a sound like a coin on a counter.

> light lantern
You light the Great Lantern.

For one breath the cracked lens throws two beams, crossed, over the whole valley. Then it lets go along the crack, and the Lantern goes dark for the last time.

[The story has ended. RESTART, RESTORE or UNDO]

Add change the lens to sound as the last line of the start block and the same commands end the other way.

> light lantern
You light the Great Lantern.

The lens gathers the flame and sends it out over the valley in one white bar. Down in the Hall, every blank page begins to fill.

You have risen to the rank of a Second-Year.

[The story has ended. RESTART, RESTORE or UNDO]

The companion file does not contain these start-block lines. They are a way of testing, and they come out again.

18.7 Killing the player

The third ending statement is for a death that is not a judgement on the whole game.

create the vial
  a thing, drinkable
  in the Yard

  A vial.

  after the player drinking
    kill the player
      It was not water. You have just long enough to be annoyed.
  end after
> drink vial
You drink the vial.

It was not water. You have just long enough to be annoyed.

[The story has ended. RESTART, RESTORE or UNDO]

kill the player takes a phrase key, or the text itself indented beneath it, and never both. It is what a deadly way from chapter 12 does for you. Use lose when the story has reached one of its endings and the player should know it. Use kill the player when they have simply walked into something, and everything chapter 12 said about fairness applies.

18.8 Build and try it

Copy relighting.story from the companion folder chapter-18 over your own file. It is the story with this chapter’s changes made, and it holds the phrases, which the chapter did not print. To make the changes by hand instead, work from this list and take the rest from that file: add use scoring and its ranks to the header, define counter workings near the top, the three score lines and their award lines, the raise workings line in each page’s first arm, the counter on the bell rope, and the after clause on the Great Lantern. There are four new phrases.

sharpee compose relighting.story --check
sharpee play .

Ten of the twenty-five points can be earned in the story as it stands, five for the page and five for the candles.

> score
You have scored 10 out of 25, earning you the rank of a Second-Year.

Under the Hood: the score is a ledger · Story IR scores, ranks

In the compiled IR every award is one entry under scores, with its name and its worth, and the ranks are a list sorted by threshold. The platform keeps a ledger of which awards have been paid, and the number the player sees is the sum of that ledger. That is why an award cannot be paid twice, and why the maximum needs no line of yours: it is the sum of the list.

From another system: scoring and endings

Inform 10 keeps a score variable that you add to, a maximum score that you set by hand, and a table of rankings, and it ends the game with “end the story” or “end the story finally”. TADS 3 awards points through Achievement objects and ends with finishGameMsg. Chord’s score lines are nearest to the TADS approach, in that each award is a named thing, and TADS can be told to give it only once (awardPointsOnce). The maximum is computed for you, and an ending is one of three plain statements.

18.9 What you have so far

The story counts, keeps score and can be won or lost. What it cannot do is change by itself. Every line you have written so far runs because the player typed something. The last chapter of Part III is about things that happen whether the player acts or not.
