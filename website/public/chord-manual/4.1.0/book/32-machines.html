32 Machines

A thing with states, from chapter 13, changes when a clause on that thing says change. That is the right tool for a page that is faded, legible or learned, where one action moves one thing one step.

Some mechanisms are not like that. A lock with a sequence, a lift with floors, a ritual in four parts: each has a state of its own that belongs to no single thing, and what the player does to one part depends on where the whole has got to. You can build these from states and clauses, and by the third state the logic is spread across six blocks and nobody can see it.

A machine gathers that logic into one block. It lists the states, and under each state the things that move the machine out of it. This chapter builds one: the shutters of the Great Lantern, which have to be cranked open before a flame in the Lantern can be seen by anybody.

32.1 The parts

A machine is not a thing. It has no description and the player cannot refer to it. It watches things, so the things come first.

create the shutter
  aka shutters, leaves
  a thing, scenery
  in the Lantern Gallery
  states: closed-round, drawn

  A shutter of brass leaves that closes round the Lantern like a bud,
  worked by a crank on the drum.

  phrase detail while the shutter is closed-round:
    The leaves are shut, and green where they overlap.

  phrase detail while the shutter is drawn:
    The leaves are folded back all the way round.

create the shutter crank
  aka crank, handle
  a thing, scenery
  in the Lantern Gallery

  An iron crank on the side of the drum, with a wooden grip worn to the
  shape of a hand that is not yours.

The shutter has two states because the player can see two conditions of it. The crank has no states and no clauses. Everything it does, the machine will say.

32.2 The machine

define machine shutter-gear
  role crank is the shutter crank
  starts shut

  state shut
    when turning the crank: half

  state half
    on enter
      phrase shutters-shift
    end on
    when turning the crank while the lens is in the Great Lantern: drawn
    when pushing the crank: shut
    on exit
      phrase gear-ticks
    end on

  state drawn, terminal
    on enter
      change the shutter to drawn
      phrase shutters-open
    end on
end machine

define machine is a top-level block with a name. Machines need no use line. Read the body in three parts.

A role line gives a short name to a thing the machine watches. role crank is the shutter crank lets the rest of the block say the crank. A machine may have several roles, and it may also name things directly.

The starts line names the state the machine begins in.

Each state block is one state, and its lines say what happens there. These states are the machine’s own. They are separate from the shutter’s closed-round and drawn, and a machine may have more states than anything the player can see. This one has three where the shutter has two, because the gear has a half-way notch at which the leaves have not really opened.

32.3 Moving between states

A when line is a transition. It names a trigger, then a colon, then the state to go to.

when turning the crank: half

The trigger here is an action on a role: the gerund of chapter 14 and the thing it is done to. While the machine is in the state shut, turning the crank moves it to half. In any other state this line is not in force.

> turn crank
You turn the shutter crank.

Something inside the drum takes up the slack. The leaves of the shutter part by a hand's breadth all the way round, and stop.

The first line of the reply is the platform’s. A thing that a machine watches for turning becomes a thing that can be turned, and the standard action reports it. The crank needed no trait word.

A transition may carry a guard, written with while.

when turning the crank while the lens is in the Great Lantern: drawn

The gear is interlocked with the lens seat. With the seat empty, the crank still goes round, and the machine stays where it is.

> turn crank
You turn the shutter crank.

A state may have several ways out. From half the machine also goes back.

when pushing the crank: shut
> push crank
You push the shutter crank.

The pawl ticks over one tooth.

A trigger may also be a plain condition, with no action in it. when the cistern is running: flooded moves a machine as soon as the condition becomes true, whatever the player was doing. The platform looks at such a line on every turn while the machine is in that state.

32.4 Entering and leaving

A state may have an on enter block and an on exit block. Each holds statements, as a clause does, and each runs when the machine arrives in the state or leaves it, by whichever transition.

The state half has both. Its on enter printed the leaves parting, and its on exit is the pawl. That is why the pawl ticked when the crank was pushed: the machine left half. It ticks again on the way to drawn.

> turn crank
You turn the shutter crank.

The pawl ticks over one tooth.

The crank runs free, and the shutters fold back all the way round, like a flower that has made up its mind.

Put prose and effects in on enter and on exit, and keep the when lines for the map of the machine. The benefit comes when a state gains a second way in. Its consequences are written once, on the state, and both ways in get them.

Inside a machine there is no it. A machine belongs to the story and not to a thing, so every statement names what it acts on.

32.5 Telling the world

The player cannot see a machine, and a clause on a thing does not ask a machine what state it is in. A machine makes itself felt by changing things.

state drawn, terminal
  on enter
    change the shutter to drawn
    phrase shutters-open
  end on

Arriving at drawn writes the shutter’s own state. Everything else in the story reads the shutter, in the ordinary way. The description’s phrase detail lines read it. So does the Lantern.

define trait lens-seat
  on the player sparking
    the lens must be in the Great Lantern: lantern-empty
    the shutter must be drawn: lantern-shuttered
  end on
end trait
> spark lantern
The shutters are closed round it. A flame in there would light the inside of a brass bud, and nothing else.

This is the division of labour to aim for. The machine owns the sequence. A thing owns a state the player can see. The rest of the story depends on the thing and never needs to know a machine exists.

terminal after a state’s name says the machine stops there. Once the shutters are drawn the crank turns freely and nothing more happens.

32.6 Build and try it

Copy relighting.story from the companion folder chapter-32 over your own file. It is the story with this chapter’s changes made, and it holds the phrases, which the chapter did not print. This is the first chapter of Part VI, and the copy’s story-version is 0.6.0. To make the changes by hand instead, work from this list and take the rest from that file: add the shutter and the crank to the Lantern Gallery, the machine near the top of the story, the second must line to the lens-seat trait, and four phrases.

sharpee compose relighting.story --check
sharpee play .

The last room of the winning walk now takes a little longer. Turn the crank once before the lens is seated and once after, and try to spark the Lantern in between.

Under the Hood: the state machine · Story IR machines

The compiled IR has a top-level machines list. Each machine carries its roles, its starting state, and for each state its transitions and its enter and exit bodies. A transition’s trigger is recorded as one of a few kinds: an action with the entity it is done to, or a condition. The platform’s state-machine plugin holds the current state of each machine, moves it when a trigger in that state fires, and runs the exit body of the state it leaves and the enter body of the state it reaches.

From another system: states and scenes

Inform 10 has no construct called a machine. Its nearest tools are a kind of value for the states (“The shutter-gear is a kind of value. The shutter-gears are shut, half and drawn”) with rules that test and set it, and scenes, which begin and end on conditions and can run rules when they do. Chord’s block differs in putting every transition of a mechanism in one place, grouped by the state it leaves.

32.7 What you have so far

The Great Lantern is now a mechanism in three parts: a lens in a seat, shutters drawn back by a crank, and a word. The interlock means the lens must be seated before the shutters will open fully, and the shutters must be open before the word will take. A player who arrives on the roof with a mended lens has perhaps six commands still to find.

The lens itself is as wide as a cartwheel, and so far Ansel has carried it up the tower stair under one arm. The next chapter has an opinion about that.
