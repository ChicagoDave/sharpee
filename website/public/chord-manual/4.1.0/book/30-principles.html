30 Principles

In the last chapter Hobb kept one story back from a stranger. The row that kept it back said so in as many words.

about "last night", "that night", "master":
  refuse when Hobb feels wary of Ansel: hobb-not-to-strangers
  phrase hobb-on-last-night when dimming was discussed

That works, and it is the author deciding. The row knows that this question is delicate, knows which feeling matters, and has a refusal written for the case. A character with twenty things to keep quiet about needs twenty such lines, and each is a place to get the rule slightly different.

Chord lets you say the rule once, about the person. Hobb does not betray a confidence. What he was told in confidence is marked as such where he knows it. Whether he tells is then worked out by the platform each time he is asked, from what he holds to and how he feels about the person asking. This chapter is about the lines that say what a person holds to.

30.1 Something told in confidence

A knows line may end with one more word.

knows night, told, certain, confided

confided says the knowledge was given in trust. It changes nothing by itself. A person with no principles tells a confided thing as readily as any other.

The name matters here in a way it did not in the last chapter. The platform connects a fact to a topic row by name: the fact is night, and the row that speaks of it must have night as one of its spellings.

about "night", "last night", "that night", "master":
  phrase hobb-on-last-night when dimming was discussed

The row has lost its refuse line. It now says only what Hobb would say, and when the subject may come up at all. Whether he says it is no longer the row’s business.

30.2 A thing a person will not do

never betrays a confidence

A never line goes in the person’s block. It names an act the person holds it wrong to commit. There are seven: betrays a confidence, lies, harms, steals, breaks a promise, abandons and trespasses. Write the act in the third person, as it reads after the person’s name.

With that line, a question that would have Hobb reveal a confided thing meets his principle, and the principle wins.

> ask hobb about last night
Hobb gives that some thought, and gives it back.

Nothing in the row refused. The platform judged that answering would be the forbidden act, and withheld the row. What the player reads is the story’s line for a subject a person has nothing to say about, the one The Relighting overrode in chapter 27. From outside, a kept confidence and plain ignorance look the same, which is the point of keeping one.

A principle may have an exception, written after a comma.

never betrays a confidence, except Ansel

The exception names a person, or a kind of person such as a servant, or anyone. A person with this line tells Ansel and no one else.

30.3 What decides between principles

One rule and no exceptions gives a character who never tells. People are more interesting when two things they hold to pull against each other, and Chord has a word for how a person settles that.

The platform recognises five forces that can move a person: fear, desire, duty, honor and love. A principle is a duty. A feeling of trust or devotion toward someone is love. When two forces disagree about an act, the stronger one wins, unless the person has a temperament line that ranks them.

temperament love over duty

A temperament is one or more pairs, each <force> over <force>, joined with and. This one says that where love and duty collide, love decides.

Here is Hobb in full.

create Hobb
  a person, proper
  pronouns he
  in the Lodge
  carries the hush page
  mood calm
  feels wary of Ansel
  knows night, told, certain, confided
  never betrays a confidence
  temperament love over duty

While he is wary of Ansel there is no love in the question, and duty is unopposed. He keeps the confidence, as above. Then the spectacles are given, mended or not, and the clause from the last chapter changes his feeling to trusts.

> give spectacles to hobb
You give the spectacles to Hobb.

Hobb hooks them over his ears and looks at you properly for the first time. "Ansel," he says, after a while. "You were always late."

> ask hobb about last night
He takes his time, of which he has had a great deal.

Hobb is quiet for some time. "Somebody had to put the Lantern out," he says. "It was guttering. The Master said a dead light was worse than none. He didn't say who." He looks at his hands.

Nothing in the row changed between those two answers. The row never mentioned trust. Hobb told because he is a man who puts a person he trusts above a rule he was given, and the story said so in one line of his block.

The puzzle is the one the last chapter had, and the player solves it the same way. What has changed is where the rule lives. Give Hobb a second confidence tomorrow, with a row of its own, and it is kept and given up on exactly the same terms, with nothing more to write.

30.4 Honesty

A second line of the same family pulls the other way.

answers honestly

A person who answers honestly holds it a duty to answer what they are asked. Alone, with no never line, it changes little: such a person tells a confided thing to whoever asks.

Its use is in the collision. A person with both lines has two duties, one forbidding the answer and one requiring it, and a temperament ranks forces and not two duties against each other. The platform’s answer to that is the human one. The person evades.

create Cy
  a person, proper
  in the Yard
  mood calm
  knows secret, told, certain, confided
  never betrays a confidence
  answers honestly

  Cy.
> ask cy about secret
Cy says, "I don't know anything about that."

And a feeling can close a mouth as well as open one. A person who answers honestly and has no rule against telling still keeps a confidence from someone they hate.

30.5 What the lines add up to

Seven people were each told the same secret in confidence, and each was asked for it by the player. This is what they did.

The person’s lines Asked, they
no principle tell
never betrays a confidence keep it
never betrays a confidence, except the asker tell
never betrays a confidence, temperament love over duty, and devoted to the asker tell
answers honestly tell
answers honestly, and hates the asker keep it
never betrays a confidence and answers honestly evade

Read down the table and you have a small cast: the gossip, the vault, the confidant, the friend, the honest witness, the enemy, and the person in a bind. Each is two or three lines.

Principles of this kind are worth their cost when a story has several people and several secrets. For one secret and one keeper, the last chapter’s refuse when is shorter and says what you mean. The gain comes with the second secret, and with the first time you change a character’s mind about someone and find that everything they would and would not tell has moved with it.

30.6 Build and try it

Copy relighting.story from the companion folder chapter-30 over your own file. It is the story with this chapter’s changes made, and it holds the row about Hobb’s spectacles, which the chapter did not print. To make the changes by hand instead, work from this list and take the rest from that file. Change Hobb’s knows line and add the two lines beneath it. Take the refuse line out of his row about the last night and give the row the spelling night. Add a row about his spectacles, so that the player has something to go on.

sharpee compose relighting.story --check
sharpee play .

Ask Hobb about the Dimming, then about the last night, then about his spectacles.

> ask hobb about spectacles
He turns the kettle a quarter round on its hook, to no purpose.

"Lost them in the spring," says Hobb. "Since then a face is a face. There are things I was told that I don't tell to a face."

Under the Hood: the arbiter · Story IR character.principles, character.temperaments

The compiled IR records a person’s never lines under character.principles and the temperament under character.temperaments, beside the knows entry that carries the confided mark. When a topic row is about to answer, the platform checks whether any spelling of the row names a fact its owner holds in confidence. If one does, it weighs the forces for and against answering: each principle, the person’s feeling toward the asker, and the temperament’s ranking. A verdict to refuse or to evade withholds the row, and nothing in the row runs.

From another system: knowledge and reticence

Neither manual describes a model of what a character will and will not reveal. The TADS 3 documentation says of its conversation system that the library does not use any sort of knowledge model for non-player characters, and suggests an isActive test on a topic to model a character’s frame of mind. That is the approach of this chapter’s first example, the refuse when on a row. Chord’s principles are a layer above it, in which the test is derived from lines about the person.

30.7 What you have so far

Hobb keeps what he was told in confidence, from anyone he does not trust, because of who he is and not because a row says so. The game plays as it did. Its porter has become someone who could keep a second secret without being taught how.
