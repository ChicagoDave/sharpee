36 Sound, music and pictures

Everything the story has said so far, it has said in text. A terminal can do nothing else, and a great deal of interactive fiction wants nothing else. A browser can do more. It can play a recording, loop a bed of sound under a scene, and put a picture on the page.

Chord treats all of that as something the story offers and the player’s program may decline. A story declares its sounds and pictures, and says when to play and show them. A client that can do it does it. A client that cannot leaves it out, and the story plays on, because nothing in the world ever depends on whether a file was heard.

This chapter gives The Relighting a recording of the bell, the sound of a flame catching, water under the skiff, and one picture.

36.1 Declaring an asset

An asset is a name the story uses and a file the client fetches.

define sound tolling from "audio/toll.mp3"

define sound lapping from "audio/lapping.mp3"

define image lantern-lit-picture from "img/lantern-lit.png"

define sound, define image and define music are top-level lines. Each gives one word as the name and one quoted path. The path is relative to a folder called assets beside the story file, so the first line above means assets/audio/toll.mp3.

The compiler checks the names and not the files. A statement that plays a sound nobody declared is an error, and so is one that shows a sound as if it were a picture. A path to a file that does not exist is not noticed until the story is built for the browser, in chapter 40. You can write this chapter’s lines, and play the story in a terminal, before any of the recordings exist.

36.2 Playing a sound

on every turn while the tower bell is tolling
  select cycling
    phrase toll-near
  or
    phrase toll-far
  or
    phrase toll-count
  end select
  raise tolls by 1
  play sound tolling
end on

play sound is a statement, and it goes wherever a statement goes. This one is the last line of the header’s on every turn block from chapter 19, so the recording is played on every turn the bell tolls.

In a terminal you will see no difference at all. The story’s own three sentences about the bell still print, and the recording is offered to a client that is not there. That is the right result, and it is why the sentences stay. A sound should never be the only way a player learns something. Some players have the sound off, some cannot hear it, and some are reading your story through a screen reader that is already talking.

36.3 A sound is also a sound in the world

Chapter 10 said that sound goes further in Chord than a loud thing, and this is where. A sound that a story plays is two things at once. It is a cue for the client, and it is a noise that happens at a place in the story’s world, where people can hear it.

The place is wherever the statement’s owner is. A play sound in a thing’s clause sounds in that thing’s room. The wisp can be made to notice.

create the wisp
  aka light
  a person, follower, listener
  pronouns it

  A scrap of light the size of a thumbnail, the same blue as the lamp
  flame. It keeps a little behind your shoulder, where a dog would.

  after hearing the tolling, once
    phrase wisp-flinches
  end after

listener is a trait word for a person. A listener is told about each sound that reaches the room it is in. after hearing the <sound> is a clause that runs when one does, and it takes once as other clauses do.

> light lamp with taper
You have no match, and you did not walk here for three days to use one. There is a word for this.

In the lodge, an old man who was not asleep opens his eyes.

You are Hobb, and that is the bell. It has not rung since the night you stopped it. There was a word for stopping it, and you wrote the word down, because you knew you would not keep it in your head, and you put the paper somewhere safe.

The tower bell tolls.

Somewhere in the Hall a small blue light goes flat and dim at the first stroke, like a flame in a draught, and creeps back to its size.

Read the last sentence again with chapter 31 in mind. On the turn the bell first tolls, the story hands the player’s role to Hobb, who is in the Lodge. The wisp is in the Library. A clause that runs after hearing prints its prose whether or not the player can see the listener, so the sentence had to be written to be true from anywhere. Write a listener’s reaction as something that could be overheard, or keep it to a change of state and let a description report it later.

36.4 What a sound sounds like

The player hears sounds too. What the player reads is up to the sound’s declaration, which may be a block.

define sound catch from "audio/catch.mp3"
  heard: The wick takes with a small dry sound, like a page turned.
end sound

A sound block closes with end sound and may hold a heard: line, a heard from afar: line, or both. The heard: line is what the player reads when the sound is made nearby. This sound belongs to the wick trait of chapter 24, so every lamp, candle and lantern in the story now catches audibly.

define trait wick
  on the player sparking
    refuse when it is lit: already-burning
    change it to lit
    phrase sparked
    play sound catch
  end on
> light lamp
You say the word. A flame stands up on the wick, blue at first, as if it had been waiting there with its coat on.

Something small and blue comes loose from the flame and hangs in the air beside it, undecided. Then it decides.

Somewhere above you, old timber takes a weight it had forgotten.

The wick takes with a small dry sound, like a page turned.

A sound prints only what its declaration states. The tolling has no block, so it prints nothing, and the story’s own bell sentences are not doubled. Give a sound a heard: line when the sound is the news. Leave it off when some other prose already carries the news and the recording is decoration.

36.5 From afar, and with no file

A sound does not need a recording. Leave out from and the sound exists in the world only: listeners hear it, the player reads its lines, and no client is asked to play anything.

define sound prising
  heard: Iron shrieks on iron, close by, and stops.
  heard from afar: Somewhere in the Hall, iron complains about iron.
end sound

When the bell begins, Tamsin Vey goes to the grille on the Bell Stair and tries her pry bar on it. Chapter 26 gave her that goal. The grille can answer for it.

create the iron grille
  aka grille
  a thing, scenery, ward
  in the Bell Stair
  states: standing, lifted

  after Tamsin Vey pushing
    play sound prising
  end after

The clause is on the grille, so the sound is made in the Bell Stair. What the player reads depends on how far away the player is. In the same room, or one open doorway from it, the sound arrives whole and the heard: line prints. Further off, or through a closed door, it arrives weakened, and the heard from afar: line prints. Far enough away, nothing prints.

The block below is what the player reads on walking from the Cloister into the Bell Stair on the turn she pushes. That one turn is easy to miss. Wait in the Cloister instead and the last line prints by itself, after a wait.

> north
Bell Stair
The foot of the tower. A stair winds up around the well where the bell rope hangs. The cloister is south. An iron grille stands across its third step.

You can see Tamsin Vey here.

The bell tolls again, and the rooks go up off the roof.

The wisp follows you.

Tamsin Vey pushes the iron grille.

Iron shrieks on iron, close by, and stops.

Waiting in the Library, two rooms from it:

> wait
You wait.

The bell tolls again, and the rooks go up off the roof.

Somewhere in the Hall, iron complains about iron.

A sound that states only one of the two lines prints nothing at the other distance. That is often what you want. A match being struck has a heard: line and no business being heard from the next county.

36.6 A bed of sound

A sound effect plays once and is over. An ambient bed loops until the story stops it.

create the skiff
  aka boat, heron
  a vehicle
  in the Mere at 0, 2

  after the player entering
    play ambient lapping
  end after

  after the player exiting
    stop ambient
  end after

play ambient takes the name of a sound asset, which must have a file. stop ambient stops it. Nothing prints for either, and nobody in the world hears a bed: it is for the player’s ears only, and it says nothing that the description of the Mere has not said.

36.7 Showing a picture

after the player sparking
  lose lens-shatters when not lantern-ready
  change the story to awake when lantern-ready
  award relit when lantern-ready
  phrase lantern-lit when lantern-ready
  show image lantern-lit-picture when lantern-ready
end after

show image puts a declared image on the page, and hide image takes it away. Like every statement, each may end in a when condition. The picture of the lit Lantern stays up for the crossing of the Mere, and the beacon’s clause hides it on the last turn.

after the player sparking
  award answered
  hide image
  win beacon-lit
end after

Where the picture appears on the page, and how large, is the page’s business. That is the next chapter.

36.8 Music, named beds and layers

The Relighting has no music. A story that does declares it the same way and has two statements for it.

define music waltz from "audio/waltz.mp3"
define sound rain from "audio/rain.mp3"
define sound hiss from "audio/hiss.mp3"
define image portrait from "img/portrait.png"
define image fog from "img/fog.png"

define ambient weather
define layer window

create the gramophone
  a thing, scenery, switchable
  in the Parlour

  A gramophone with a horn like a brass lily.

  after the player switching_on
    play music waltz looping
    play ambient hiss
    phrase needle-down when not client has music
  end after

  after the player switching_off
    stop music
    stop ambient
  end after

play music starts a track, and looping makes it repeat. stop music ends it. There is one music track at a time.

There may be several beds. play ambient hiss uses the bed every story has, which is called main. define ambient weather declares a second bed by name, and play ambient rain in weather and stop ambient in weather address it. The two loop together and stop separately.

Pictures have layers in the same way. Three exist without being declared: background, main and overlay. show image with no layer uses main. define layer window adds one, and show image fog in window shows a picture in it.

create the curtains
  a thing, scenery, openable
  in the Parlour

  Heavy curtains.

  after the player opening
    play ambient rain in weather
    show image fog in window
    show image portrait
  end after

  after the player closing
    stop ambient in weather
    transition fade
    clear
  end after

Two more statements are for the page as a whole. transition followed by a word asks the client for a visual transition of that kind, and clear asks it to clear the screen.

36.9 Asking what the client can do

The gramophone’s clause has a condition in it that reads the client and not the world.

phrase needle-down when not client has music

client has is followed by one word from a closed list of capabilities, among them sound, music, images, transitions, layers, split-pane, status-bar and sidebar. It is true when the program the player is using has said it can do that thing. In a terminal the gramophone prints its sentence, because a terminal has no music.

> switch on gramophone
The gramophone hums to life.

The needle comes down, and a waltz gets up out of the crackle.

This is the pattern for anything a recording was going to carry alone: play the recording, and say the same thing in words to a player who has no recording.

One statement may not be made to depend on the client. play sound hiss when client has sound is an error, analysis.play-sound-client-has. A played sound happens in the world, where listeners react to it, and the world must not go differently for two players because one of them has speakers. Leave the condition off. A client without sound does not play the cue, and that is all.

36.10 Build and try it

Copy relighting.story and the assets folder from the companion folder chapter-36 into your project, the story over your own file and the folder beside it. The story is yours with this chapter’s changes made, and the folder holds three short recordings and a picture. To make the changes by hand instead, work from this list and take the rest from that file: add the five declarations at the top of the story, play sound catch to the wick trait, play sound tolling to the header’s block, the clause to the grille, listener and its clause to the wisp, the two clauses to the skiff, and the show image and hide image lines.

sharpee compose relighting.story --check
sharpee play .

Light the lamp and read its last line. Then wait in the Library for the bell, look in the newspapers as Hobb, and keep waiting as Ansel until iron complains about iron. Nothing will play in the terminal. Chapter 40 builds the page where it does.

Under the Hood: media events · event ids media.sound.play, media.ambient.play, media.image.show

Each statement in this chapter becomes one event with a small payload. play sound tolling sends media.sound.play with the file’s path as src. play ambient lapping sends media.ambient.play with the path and the bed’s name, and show image sends media.image.show with the path and, when one is named, the layer. The stops and the hide are media.ambient.stop, media.music.stop and media.image.hide. A played sound also raises the world’s own sound event, which carries the thing that made it, the room, and the sound’s name, and it is that event a listener’s after hearing clause answers. A test tree can assert on any of these by name, as chapter 38 shows.

From another system: figures and sounds

Inform 10 declares a picture with a sentence such as Figure of Woodlands is the file "Woodlands.png". and shows it with display the Figure of Woodlands;. A sound is Sound of rustling leaves is the file "Rustling leaves.ogg". and play the sound of rustling leaves;. Writing with Inform says both can be used only when the project is compiled to the Glulx story file format. TADS 3 does it in the output text, with HTML TADS tags: <IMG SRC="picture.jpg"> for a picture and a <SOUND> tag for a sound, and a game asks systemInfo() what the interpreter can do. In neither system is a played sound an event in the story’s world that a character can hear, on the pages read for this comparison.

36.11 What you have so far

The Relighting can be heard. The bell has a recording and the wisp flinches at its first stroke. A flame catching makes a sound the player reads. A pry bar on the grille is heard plainly from the Cloister and faintly from the Library. The skiff has water under it, and the lit Lantern has a picture.

None of it has been heard or seen yet, because a terminal cannot. The picture needs a place on a page, and the page needs to be told which of its features this story uses. The next chapter is about the page.
