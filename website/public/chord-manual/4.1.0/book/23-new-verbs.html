23 New verbs

The craft of Lanternhall calls its spells workings. A working is a single spoken word: spark, mend, hush, unbind. Ansel learns one by reading its page, and then says it to a thing. None of those four words is a command the platform knows.

This chapter adds them. A new verb in Chord is a new action, and you declare one with define action. By the end of the chapter all four workings can be typed, each asks for its page, and each then finds that nothing answers it. That last part is not a failure of the chapter. It is the most important idea in it.

23.1 Declaring an action

define action mending
  grammar
    mend the target
  the target must be reachable
  the mend page must be learned: no-such-word
  otherwise refuse nothing-to-mend

The head line names the action. An action’s name is a gerund, the same “-ing” word that will later appear in clause heads as on the player mending. The block has no end line. It runs until the indentation stops.

The four lines beneath are four different kinds of line, and each gets a section.

23.2 The grammar

The grammar block lists what the player may type, one pattern to a line. You have read patterns before, in the passage of chapter 12. They have four devices.

Written Means
mend a word the player types
the target a slot: a place where the player names a thing
mend or fix either word
[the] a word that may be left out

A slot is the followed by a name you choose. target is the usual name for the one thing a verb is done to. The name matters only inside the action, where the other lines use it to talk about whatever the player named.

An action may have several patterns, and a pattern may have no slot at all.

define action sparking
  grammar
    spark the target
    spark

Now spark lamp and plain spark are both understood. The second needs a line to say what happens when the thing is missing.

  refuse without target: working-wants-a-thing
> spark
A working is said to something. You say it to the air, and the air has heard it all before.

One limit applies to every pattern you write. It may not be a command the standard grammar already has.

probe.story:37:1 error [analysis.repeats-standard-grammar] `poke :target` is already the standard `touching` grammar, so this action would take that command from it. To give a thing its own response, write `on the player touching` in that thing's block or its trait's. To add new wording, use `extend action touching`. To replace the standard action, first remove its shape with `remove from action touching`.

The message gives the three ways out, and chapter 25 is about the second and third. Before you define an action, look in Appendix C for the verb. The standard grammar is larger than most authors expect.

23.3 What must be true of the thing named

  the target must be reachable

A line of this form, with no colon and no phrase, says how close the named thing has to be. There are three words for it.

Word The thing must be
visible in view
reachable near enough to touch
held in the player’s hands

When the thing is not there, the platform says so in its own words. Hushing uses visible, because a bell at the top of a tower can be spoken to and cannot be touched.

> hush bell
You can't see any bell here.

That was typed in the Library. In the Bell Stair, the bell is in view.

23.4 What must be true of the player

  the mend page must be learned: no-such-word

This is the must line of chapter 15, and it means what it meant there. It is on the action, and not on any thing, because it concerns the person speaking. Ansel cannot say a word that has not been read, whatever it is said to.

> mend lamp
The shape of it is there, as a name is on the tip of the tongue. The word is not.

An action may also carry refuse when lines, and statements of its own, as a later section shows. All of its refusals and requirements stand above its first statement.

23.5 What an action may not ask

Here is the rule that shapes everything else. An action never asks which thing was named.

You cannot write, in the mending action, that if the target is the lens then the crack closes. Chord has no way to say it. If you try, with a line such as refuse when the target is the kettle, the compiler refuses it by name, as analysis.slot-identity-test.

The reason is the habit this book has pressed since chapter 14. What happens to the lens belongs in the lens’s block. If the mending action listed the things it could mend, then adding a mendable thing to the story would mean editing the action, and the facts about the lens would be in two places.

So an action knows three things: what the player may type, what must be true of the player, and what to say when nothing answers. Everything else belongs to the thing.

23.6 When nothing answers

  otherwise refuse nothing-to-mend

When the player names a thing, the action asks that thing whether it has an answer. If it has none, this line speaks.

At the end of this chapter no thing in Lanternhall has an answer to any of the four workings. So once the page has been read, every one of them misses.

> spark lamp
There is nothing in that for a flame to stand on.

Somewhere above you, old timber takes a weight it had forgotten.

> spark grimoire
There is nothing in that for a flame to stand on.

A slow draught moves through the Hall, one way and then the other.

The first of those is plainly wrong, and it is wrong in an instructive way. The action is complete. The lamp has not yet been told that it answers to sparking. Telling it is the whole of the next chapter.

A miss can name the thing the player typed. The slot’s name works as a marker.

define phrases en-US
  nothing-to-tune:
    {capitalize the target} is as well tuned as it will ever be.

23.7 The four workings

Here are the other three.

define action sparking
  grammar
    spark the target
    spark
  refuse without target: working-wants-a-thing
  the target must be reachable
  the spark page must be learned: no-such-word
  otherwise refuse nothing-to-spark

define action hushing
  grammar
    hush the target
    hush
  refuse without target: working-wants-a-thing
  the target must be visible
  the hush page must be learned: no-such-word
  otherwise refuse nothing-to-hush

define action unbinding
  grammar
    unbind the target
    unbind
  refuse without target: working-wants-a-thing
  the target must be reachable
  the unbind page must be learned: no-such-word
  otherwise refuse nothing-to-unbind

Mending takes the same two extra lines. The four blocks are nearly identical, and Chord offers no way to write one and stamp out four. That is a real cost, and the answer for now is that four short blocks are easy to read and easy to keep in step.

23.8 A verb that only answers

Not every verb is done to a thing. Some are simply said. There is a fifth word in this country that every child knows and that nobody at Lanternhall ever managed to cast.

define action blessing
  grammar
    gnusto
    say gnusto
    gnusto the target
  refuse when the tower bell is tolling: gnusto-drowned
  phrase gnusto-said

This action has a body. After its refusals come ordinary statements, and they run whenever the action succeeds. There is no otherwise refuse, because nothing needs to answer. The action answers for itself.

> gnusto
"Gnusto," you say, as everybody does, and the morning is exactly as bright as it was. It is a word about sunlight. The scholars here always said it had been a working once, somewhere over the water, and could never say what it did.

Use a body for a verb that is about the player or the story: praying, singing, counting the money. An action’s body may do anything a clause can do. It may change the story’s state, raise a counter or award a score, and an action may declare a score line of its own. It still may not ask what was named.

The third pattern, gnusto the target, lets a player try the word on a thing. Nothing answers, and the body runs all the same.

23.9 Verbs with a subject

Some verbs take a subject and not a thing: remember the fire, think about home. The subject may be something that is not in the room, or not a thing at all. A slot can be declared a topic, and then the action answers by subject, with about lines.

define action remembering
  grammar
    remember the subject
    recall the subject
  the subject is a topic
  about "dimming":
    phrase remember-dimming
  about "gnusto":
    phrase remember-gnusto
  about Hobb:
    phrase remember-hobb
  otherwise refuse remember-nothing

An about line names a subject and holds the statements to run for it. A subject in quotes is matched against the words the player typed. A subject without quotes is a thing or a person in the story, matched by any of its names.

> remember dimming
You remember it as a night when every lamp in the Hall went out at once, and a morning when nobody could make one light.

> remember the dimming
You remember it as a night when every lamp in the Hall went out at once, and a morning when nobody could make one light.

> recall hobb
He confiscated a kettle from you in your second week. You have a feeling it is the same kettle.

> remember breakfast
Nothing comes. Thirty years is a long time.

The article is dropped before matching, so "dimming" answers both of the first two commands. Write each subject once, without “the”. A quoted line may list several spellings with commas between them, as in about "dimming", "night".

This does not break the rule about asking what was named. The rule is about things in the world, which have blocks of their own to answer in. A subject has no block. The about lines are where its answer lives.

You will meet about lines again in chapter 27, where they are what a person says when asked about something. An action with a topic is the same machinery pointed at the player’s own head.

23.10 Other kinds of slot

Two more lines describe a slot.

the words takes the rest of the line makes a slot swallow everything the player types after the verb. It is for writing, saying and naming.

define action scrawling
  grammar
    scrawl the words
  the words takes the rest of the line
  phrase scrawled

The text is available to a phrase as {verbatim:words}.

> scrawl hello there world
[scrawled: hello there world]

the tool is an instrument marks a second slot as the thing the action is done with, as in a pattern tune the target with the tool. The standard grammar uses it for keys, weapons and tools.

23.11 A verb that comes and goes

An action’s head may carry a condition. The verb then exists only while the condition holds.

define action shouting while the tower bell is tolling
  grammar
    shout
  phrase shout-lost

Before the bell begins, the story does not know the word.

> shout
I don't understand that.

Once it is tolling:

> shout
You shout. The bell tolls through the middle of it.

This is stronger than a refusal. A refused verb tells the player that the command exists. A verb that is not understood tells them nothing. Use it when the existence of the command would itself give something away, and otherwise prefer a refuse when with a sentence that helps. The Relighting does not use this action. It is here as an example.

23.12 Why a thing cannot simply answer

You may already have tried the obvious thing: an on the player mending clause in the lens’s block, like the on the player reading clause on each page.

probe.story:19:17 error [analysis.story-action-on-clause] `on the player thumping` in `drum`'s block would never run: `thumping` is this story's own action, and it runs a thing's `after` clauses and the `on` clauses of its traits, never a thing's own `on` clause. To react once the action succeeds, write `after the player thumping`. To refuse or answer in the action's place, move the clause into a trait and give `drum` that trait.

For a standard action, a thing’s own on clause is how it answers. For an action you define, the answer comes from a trait. The message says what to do, and the next chapter does it.

23.13 Build and try it

Copy relighting.story from the companion folder chapter-23 over your own file. It is the story with this chapter’s changes made, and it holds the phrases, which the chapter did not print in full. To make the changes by hand instead, work from this list and take the rest from that file: add the four working actions, the blessing action and the remembering action near the top of the file, and add their eleven phrases.

sharpee compose relighting.story --check
sharpee play .

Try each working before and after its page. The lamp still lights with the standard light lamp, as it has since chapter 15. Spark is a word now, and has nothing to work on.

Under the Hood: an action is data · Story IR actions

In the compiled IR the sparking action is an entry of plain data. It has a name, and then its patterns, each a run of words and slots; its constraints, which are the must be reachable lines; its musts, each a condition and a phrase key; its refusals, which are the refuse lines; otherwise, which holds the phrase key of the otherwise refuse line; scores, which is empty for this action; and its body. A last field, span, records where in the file the action was written. There is no code in it. The platform has one piece of machinery that runs any action described this way, and that machinery is what looks for an answer on the thing and falls back to your otherwise refuse line.

From another system: new actions

In Inform 10 you write “Mending is an action applying to one touchable thing” and “Understand ‘mend [something]’ as mending”, and then rules say what it does, including rules that name particular things. In TADS 3 you define the action with DefineTAction and a VerbRule, and each object answers in its dobjFor(Mend) block. Chord follows the TADS division more strictly than TADS does. The action holds grammar and what is true of the player, the thing holds the response, and the compiler will not let either do the other’s work.

23.14 What you have so far

The story understands four workings, one old word about sunlight, and Ansel’s memory. It knows who may say each working, and how near the thing must be. It does not yet know what any working does. That knowledge belongs to the lamp, the lens, the bell and the grille, and the next chapter gives it to them.
