2 The story header

Every story begins by saying what it is. In Chord that is the job of the story block, which this book calls the header. It holds the facts about the story as a whole: what it is called, who wrote it, which version this is, and the words the player reads before anything else happens.

Here is the header of The Relighting again.

story
  title: The Relighting
  id: relighting
  story-version: 0.1.0
  authors:
    The Sharpee Project
  description: Thirty years after the magic went out of the world, a lamp burns blue.
  prologue:
    You were here for one term, thirty years ago, and you would have said
    you remembered none of it. Then last week a candle leaned toward your
    hand, and you started walking.

The first line is the single word story. Everything indented beneath it is a field: a key, a colon, and a value. The header takes a fixed set of fields and no others, so you cannot invent one. This chapter covers the eight that describe the story. The rest belong to later chapters, most of them to Part VII, where the story is given its look in a browser.

2.1 The two fields every header needs

A header must have a title: and an authors: list. Nothing else is required. This is the smallest header the compiler accepts:

story
  title: The Relighting
  authors:
    The Sharpee Project

The title is everything after the colon, to the end of the line. It is not quoted, and it may contain any punctuation you like.

The authors are a list, even when there is only one. The key stands on a line by itself, and each name goes on its own line beneath it, indented one level further. A second author is a second line:

  authors:
    Hobb
    Tamsin Vey

You may not put a name on the same line as authors:. The compiler asks for the list form:

relighting.story:3:3 error [parse.header-inline-list] `authors:` takes no inline name — list one name per line, indented under `authors:`.

Leave either field out and the compiler names the one that is missing:

relighting.story:1:1 error [parse.story-title] The story block requires a `title:` field.

2.2 What the player sees

When a story starts, the player is shown the title, the description if there is one, the authors and the version, and then the first room. With the smallest header above, and a first room cut down to one sentence so that the header’s lines stand out, the opening looks like this. It is here to be read. Nothing in this chapter is built.

The Relighting
By The Sharpee Project
Story v0.0.0

Lodge
The porter's lodge is one small room.

With two authors the second line reads By Hobb, Tamsin Vey. The version reads 0.0.0 because the header did not give one. The next section fixes that.

2.3 Version, description and credits

story-version: is the version of your story, written as three numbers separated by full stops. It is yours to count however you please. This book gives The Relighting the version 0.1.0 at the end of Part I and raises the middle number with each Part.

description: is one line that says what the story is about. It appears under the title when the story starts, and again whenever the player types about.

testers: is a second list of names, written exactly like authors:. It is a place to record the people who played your story before it was finished.

With the version and the description added, typing about prints this:

> about
The Relighting
Version 0.1.0
By The Sharpee Project

Thirty years after the magic went out of the world, a lamp burns blue.

2.4 The prologue

The prologue is the text the player reads first, before the title. It is where a story sets its scene.

  prologue:
    You were here for one term, thirty years ago, and you would have said
    you remembered none of it. Then last week a candle leaned toward your
    hand, and you started walking.

The key stands alone on its line and the text follows beneath it, indented one level further. This is prose, so it wraps wherever you like and the player sees one paragraph. A blank line starts a second paragraph. The opening of The Relighting now reads:

You were here for one term, thirty years ago, and you would have said you remembered none of it. Then last week a candle leaned toward your hand, and you started walking.

The Relighting
Thirty years after the magic went out of the world, a lamp burns blue.
By The Sharpee Project
Story v0.1.0

Lodge
The porter's lodge is one small room built into the thickness of the gate arch. A chair, a kettle and thirty years of newspapers have filled most of it. On the sill a lamp is burning with a steady blue flame.

A short value may also sit on the same line as its key, which is how description: is written above. Both description: and prologue: accept either form.

There is one trap in the short form. If the value is a single word made of letters and hyphens, such as prologue: opening-words, the compiler does not print that word. It takes it as the name of a phrase, which is a piece of text defined elsewhere in the file, and it reports an error if no phrase has that name. Chapter 20 teaches phrases. Until then, write your prologue as a sentence and the trap cannot catch you.

2.5 The two identifiers

id: is a short name for the story, written in lower-case letters with hyphens between the words. The player never sees it. The tools use it to name the things they make from your story, such as the folder that holds its tests and the folder that holds its browser build. Give every story an id:, and do not change it once you have chosen it.

ifid: is the story’s Interactive Fiction Identifier, a long code that identifies one work of interactive fiction to archives and catalogues. You do not need to write one. The Relighting’s header has none, and chapter 40 shows where the build puts the one it makes for you.

Under the Hood: the header in the Story IR · sharpee compose

The header compiles to the meta section of the Story IR. The title is its own entry, and every other field you wrote is collected under fields:

"meta": {
  "title": "The Relighting",
  "fields": {
    "authors": [
      "The Sharpee Project"
    ],
    "testers": [],
    "themes": []
  }
}

That is the smallest header from earlier in this chapter. A field you did not write is simply absent, which is why the version was printed as 0.0.0.

2.6 When a field is refused

Because the set of fields is fixed, a key the header does not know is an error, and the error lists every key it does know:

relighting.story:5:3 error [parse.header-unknown-field] Unknown story-header field `genre:` — the header takes exactly: title, authors, testers, ifid, id, story-version, prologue, description, client, theme, template, themes, default-theme, storage-prefix, publish-source, auto-assertion (plus states/score/use/on lines).

The words in parentheses at the end are not fields. They are other kinds of line that a header may hold, and each has its own chapter: states in chapter 13, score and use in chapter 18, and on in chapter 19.

Three keys from earlier versions of Chord are refused by name, and the error for each one tells you its replacement. version: is now story-version:, blurb: is now description:, and by: is now authors:.

The compiler is strict about which fields exist and relaxed about what you put in them. If you write title: twice, the second one wins and nothing warns you. An id: with spaces or capital letters in it is accepted as written. It is worth reading your header once more before you move on.

From another system: bibliographic data

Inform 10 lets the first line of the source give the title and the author, as an abbreviation, and sets the rest by sentences about the story, such as its description and its release number. TADS 3 keeps the same facts as properties of one object that describes the game. Chord’s header is nearer to the TADS 3 arrangement, with one difference worth knowing early. The header is a closed form. You fill in the fields it has, and a fact it has no field for belongs somewhere else in the story.

2.7 What you have so far

Your relighting.story begins with a header that names the story, credits its author, gives it a version and a description, and sets the scene. The header says nothing yet about who the player is. That is the next chapter.
