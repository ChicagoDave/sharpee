24 New kinds of thing

The last chapter ended with four verbs and nothing to use them on. The sparking action knows who may spark. It does not know what a flame does to a lamp, and it has been forbidden to find out.

That knowledge goes in a trait. You have used traits since chapter 6 without defining one: openable, wearable and light-source are trait words, and each makes a thing answer some standard action. A trait of your own makes a thing answer an action of your own. This chapter defines five of them, and at the end of it The Relighting can be won.

24.1 A trait that answers a verb

Three things in Lanternhall take a flame: the hand lamp, the refectory candles and the Great Lantern. What happens when one of them is sparked is the same in each case, so it is written once.

define trait wick
  on the player sparking
    refuse when it is lit: already-burning
    change it to lit
    phrase sparked
  end on
end trait

A trait is a block that opens with define trait and a name and closes with end trait. Inside it is a clause, of exactly the kind you have been writing in create blocks since chapter 14. The difference is the word it.

A clause in a thing’s block names the thing, because the block has one owner. A trait has many, or will have. So inside a trait, it means whichever thing has the trait and is being acted on. This is the one place in Chord where it is allowed, as chapter 15 promised.

A thing takes the trait by adding its name to the kind line.

create the hand lamp
  a thing, light-source, starts unlit, wick
  in the Lodge

  A tin hand lamp with a wire handle. Its wick is dry and its glass is
  clean, which is Hobb all over.

  phrase detail while the hand lamp is lit:
    It is burning with a small flame, and the flame is blue.

Your trait word sits beside the platform’s, and reads the same way. The lamp’s own on the player lighting clause, with its requirement about the page, is gone. The sparking action carries that requirement now.

> spark lamp
You say the word. A flame stands up on the wick, blue at first, as if it had been waiting there with its coat on.

Somewhere above you, old timber takes a weight it had forgotten.

> spark lamp
It is burning already, and the word slides off it.

A slow draught moves through the Hall, one way and then the other.

Follow the command through, because this is the centre of the language. The player types spark lamp. The sparking action checks its own lines: the lamp is reachable and the page has been learned. It then asks the lamp for an answer. The lamp has the wick trait, and the trait has a clause for sparking, so that clause runs with it meaning the lamp. Had the player named the grimoire, there would be no such clause, and the action’s otherwise refuse line would speak.

The action never learned what a lamp is. The lamp never learned what the player must know. Each holds its own half.

The line change it to lit deserves a second look. lit is not a state you declared. It belongs to light-source, and chapter 13 said that such states are read and changed like your own. Here is where that matters. A working that could only set states of your own invention could never light a real lamp, and the Undercroft would stay dark.

24.2 One trait, several things

The candles and the Great Lantern take the same word.

create the candles
  aka prickets
  a thing, scenery, plural, light-source, starts unlit, wick
  in the Refectory
  score supper-lit worth 5

  Stubs of tallow in iron prickets down the middle of each table.

  after the player sparking
    move the unbind page here
    award supper-lit
    phrase candles-catch
  end after

The candles’ after clause used to say lighting. It now says sparking, and it is still in the candles’ own block. This is the half of the rule that the last chapter’s error message spelled out. A thing may not answer one of your actions with an on clause of its own, and it may react to one with an after clause. The trait says what sparking does to any wick. The candles say what follows when these particular wicks catch.

> spark candles
You say the word. A flame stands up on the wick, blue at first, as if it had been waiting there with its coat on.

The flame runs down the length of the table, and every cold wick takes it. In the candlelight there is a leaf of paper under the middle pricket that the daylight did not show.

The tower bell tolls.

You have risen to the rank of a Second-Year.

24.3 A trait may answer standard actions too

There is a hole in this. light lamp is a standard command, the lamp is a light-source, and the standard lighting action would light it without any word at all. Until now the lamp’s own clause stood in the way. The trait can do the same for every wick at once.

define trait wick
  on the player sparking
    refuse when it is lit: already-burning
    change it to lit
    phrase sparked
  end on

  on the player lighting
    refuse light-needs-the-word
  end on
end trait
> light lamp
You have no match, and you did not walk here for three days to use one. There is a word for this.

The tower bell strikes once, and the sound goes through the stones under your feet.

A trait may hold clauses for any actions, standard or your own, and several of each kind. The next chapter closes this hole in a better way, by teaching the story that light means spark.

24.4 A trait with one thing

The Great Lantern needs one thing more than the lamp does. It must not be sparked without its lens. That used to be a must line in the Lantern’s own on the player lighting clause, and a thing’s own on clause cannot answer sparking.

So the requirement becomes a trait, even though only the Lantern will ever have it.

define trait lens-seat
  on the player sparking
    the lens must be in the Great Lantern: lantern-empty
  end on
end trait
create the Great Lantern
  aka lantern
  a container, scenery, light-source, starts unlit, lens-seat, wick
  in the Lantern Gallery
  holds 1
  score relit worth 15

  A drum of brass and glass the height of a door, with a seat inside it
  for a lens and a wick as thick as your arm. Nothing has burned in it
  for thirty years.

  after the player sparking
    lose lens-shatters when not lantern-ready
    change the story to awake when lantern-ready
    award relit when lantern-ready
    win lantern-lit when lantern-ready
  end after

A trait with a single carrier is ordinary in Chord and nothing to be tidied away. It is how a thing answers one of your verbs in a way no other thing does.

The Lantern has two traits that answer sparking, and both of them answer. Their clauses are taken in the order the trait words stand on the kind line, in two passes. First every clause’s refusals and requirements are settled. Only when none of them refuses does any clause’s body run. So an empty Lantern is refused before any flame is lit, and that is true whichever trait is written first.

What the order decides is what the player reads. If two traits would both refuse, the refusal of the one written first is the one that is spoken. When the command goes through, the bodies run in the same order, and so do their phrases.

> spark lantern
There is a wick, and there is a seat for a lens with no lens in it. A flame here would light the roof and nothing else.

24.5 A trait with states and a score

A trait can own states. The lens has been cracked or sound since chapter 13. That pair is really a fact about anything that can be mended, so it moves into the trait.

define trait mendable
  states: cracked, sound
  score made-whole worth 5
  phrases en-US
    mended:
      You say the word and mean it. What was broken is whole, and has
      no memory of having been otherwise.

  on the player mending
    refuse when it is sound: already-whole
    change it to sound
    award made-whole
    phrase mended
  end on
end trait

Each thing with the trait gets its own copy of the states. Inside the trait they are read as it is sound. Outside, they are read by the thing’s name, as before: the lens is sound. The lantern-ready condition and the lens’s detail phrases did not have to change.

The lens loses its own states line and gains a word.

create the lens
  a thing, mendable
  in the crate

The trait also holds a score line, and a phrases block. The block declares a phrase that belongs to the trait, in the list form of define phrases, indented inside the trait. It keeps the trait’s words beside the trait’s clause.

24.6 A thing’s own words, again

A second thing in Lanternhall has been broken since chapter 6. Hobb’s spectacles have one arm held on with thread.

create the spectacles
  aka glasses
  a thing, plural, concealed, wearable, mendable
  in the Lodge

  Wire spectacles. Hobb has been looking for them since the spring.

  phrase detail while the spectacles is cracked:
    One arm has come away, and is held on with a turn of thread.

  phrase detail while the spectacles is sound:
    Both arms are sound.

One word on the kind line, and the mend working mends them.

The replies in this section were recorded with the bell already hushed, which the next section does. Mend anything while it is still tolling and the reply ends with the bell.

> mend spectacles
You say the word and mean it. What was broken is whole, and has no memory of having been otherwise.

That is the trait’s own phrase. It serves for spectacles, and it is too plain for the lens, which is the mend the whole game has been leading up to. Chapter 21 said that a phrase line in a thing’s block is that thing’s own version of a piece of text. Here is the general use it promised.

create the lens
  a thing, mendable
  in the crate

  A disc of glass as wide as a cartwheel, ground in rings.

  phrase mended:
    You say the word and mean it. The crack closes from both edges at
    once, like a mouth deciding not to speak.

The trait’s clause says phrase mended. For the spectacles that is the trait’s text. For the lens it is the lens’s.

> mend lens
You say the word and mean it. The crack closes from both edges at once, like a mouth deciding not to speak.

> examine lens
A disc of glass as wide as a cartwheel, ground in rings. There is no flaw in it anywhere, and you looked.

The score was declared once, in the trait, and it is paid once. Mending the spectacles earns the five points, and mending the lens afterwards earns nothing more. If each mend should score, give each thing a score line of its own and award it in an after the player mending clause.

24.7 The bell and the grille

Two traits remain, each with one carrier.

define trait hushable
  score quieted worth 5

  on the player hushing
    it must be tolling: already-quiet
    change it to silent
    award quieted
    phrase bell-hushed
  end on
end trait

define trait ward
  score unsealed worth 5

  on the player unbinding
    refuse when the tower bell is unrung: ward-asleep
    refuse when the tower bell is tolling: ward-in-the-bell
    change it to lifted
    move it offstage
    award unsealed
    phrase ward-lifts
  end on
end trait

The tower bell becomes a thing, scenery, hushable and the iron grille a thing, scenery, ward. Neither trait declares states. They use tolling, silent and lifted, which the bell and the grille declare themselves, and the compiler checks that every thing with the trait has the states the trait changes.

The ward trait is where the puzzle is. The grille cannot be unbound before the bell has rung, because the ward is asleep, and it cannot be unbound while the bell is tolling, because the ward is in the bell. So the bell must be hushed first, and hushing needs the third page.

> unbind grille
The word goes into the iron and comes back out on the next stroke of the bell. Whatever holds the grille is being rung into it.

The bell tolls. You have stopped counting.

> hush bell
You say the word up into the dark of the tower. The bell swings twice more without a sound, and hangs still.

> unbind grille
You say the word. The grille is not there, in the manner of a thing that was never there, and the stair goes up.

You have risen to the rank of a Journeyman.

The bell has stopped at last. Every turn since chapter 19 has ended with it. The line about rank comes on whichever of these awards takes the score to thirty. Here that was the grille, because the lamp, the candles and the other pages had been seen to first and nothing had been mended yet. In another order it follows the bell, or a mend.

The grille is moved offstage, the Bell Stair’s {grille-across} marker falls silent because its condition is no longer true, and the way up is no longer blocked. Three lines written in three different chapters agree, because all three read one state.

24.8 Data on a trait

A trait may give each of its things a value of its own, called a field. The Relighting has no need of one, so here is a brazier from another story.

define trait burner
  data
    fuel: one of tallow, oil
    keeper: optional entity

  on the player stoking
    phrase fuel-tallow when its fuel is tallow
    phrase fuel-oil when its fuel is oil
  end on
end trait

The data block lists the fields. Each is a name, a colon and a type.

Type Holds
one of and a list one word from the list
number a whole number
entity a thing, a person or a room
name a word of your choosing

A thing sets its fields on the kind line, after the trait word, with with and then and between one field and the next.

create the cresset
  a thing, burner with fuel oil
  in the Yard

create the brazier
  a thing, burner with fuel oil and keeper the stub
  in the Yard

Inside the trait a field is read as its fuel, and from outside as the brazier's keeper. A field is changed with set, as in set its brightness to 3.

Set every field on every thing that has the trait, unless the field is marked optional and the trait never reads it without checking. A field that was never set has no value to read.

Reach for a field when things with the trait differ by a value. Reach for a state when they differ by a condition that changes during play.

24.9 Is it a wick?

A trait name can be used in a condition, after is a.

define condition cold-wick: it is a wick and it is not lit

the hand lamp is a wick is true, and the kettle is a wick is false. With an open condition such as cold-wick, the any, no and each of chapter 16 can now talk about every thing of a sort you defined.

Because a trait name is used this way, it cannot be a word the language already has. You may not call a trait container or openable.

24.10 Build and try it

Copy relighting.story from the companion folder chapter-24 over your own file. It is the story with this chapter’s changes made, and it holds the phrases, which the chapter did not print in full. To make the changes by hand instead, work from this list and take the rest from that file.

Add the five traits above the actions. Give wick to the lamp, the candles and the Lantern, with lens-seat before it on the Lantern. Give mendable to the lens and the spectacles, hushable to the bell and ward to the grille. Remove the three on the player lighting clauses from the lamp, the candles and the Lantern, and change the candles’ and the Lantern’s after clauses to sparking. Remove the lens’s states line. Lengthen the rank ladder to its final form: Journeyman at 30, Lamplighter at 50. There are nine new phrases.

sharpee compose relighting.story --check
sharpee play .

The game can be played to its end for the first time. Read the first page. Spark the lamp and the candles. Find the three other pages and read them. Hush the bell, unbind the grille, mend the lens, carry it to the roof and seat it.

> spark lantern
You say the word. A flame stands up on the wick, blue at first, as if it had been waiting there with its coat on.

The lens gathers the flame and sends it out over the valley in one white bar. Down in the Hall, every blank page begins to fill.

You have risen to the rank of a Lamplighter.

[The story has ended. RESTART, RESTORE or UNDO]

Then play it again and leave the lens cracked.

Under the Hood: capability · Story IR traits

Find wick under traits in the compiled IR and look at its sparking clause. One of its fields reads "routing": "capability". That is the platform’s name for what this chapter has been doing. A trait declares that it is capable of answering an action, and the action asks every trait of the thing that answers it, in the order they are written. The standard traits answer the standard actions through the same door. Your wick and the platform’s openable are the same kind of object, which is why they sit side by side on a kind line.

From another system: kinds and classes

In Inform 10 you would say “A wick is a kind of thing” and write rules for sparking a wick. In TADS 3 you would define a Wick class and give it a dobjFor(Spark) block, and an object could list several classes. Chord’s trait is nearer to the TADS class used as a mix-in. A thing has exactly one kind, such as a thing or a container, and any number of traits. Traits do not inherit from one another. What several traits share, they share by being composed on the same thing.

24.11 What you have so far

The four workings work. A thing answers a working by having a trait, and each trait says in one place what the working does. The story can be won and lost, and the bell can be stopped.

A player who types light lamp is still told off for it, though, and one who types fix lens or sit on chair is not understood at all. The last chapter of Part IV makes the story’s grammar as forgiving as its prose.
