29 Inner lives

Hobb and Tamsin Vey can now say a great deal, and what they say depends on things: whether the bell is tolling, whether a bargain is struck, what has been asked before. All of that is the state of the world. None of it is the state of the person.

A person in Chord has an inside as well, and the language calls it the character model. You have already written one line of it, the mood calm that chapter 27 needed. This chapter covers the three parts of the model whose effects you can see and use in a story: a mood, feelings toward other people, and things known.

Each works the same way. A line in the person’s block says how they begin. A statement changes it. A condition reads it, and anything that takes a condition can then depend on it: a topic row, a manner row, a clause, a goal.

29.1 Mood

create Hobb
  a person, proper
  pronouns he
  in the Lodge
  carries the hush page
  mood calm

The mood line gives the mood a person starts in. The platform has thirteen mood words: calm, content, cheerful, nervous, anxious, panicked, angry, furious, sad, grieving, suspicious, confused and resigned.

A mood is read with is, as a state is.

define manner for Hobb
  when Hobb is calm:
    beat "He turns the kettle a quarter round on its hook, to no purpose."
    beat "He looks at the blue flame on the sill before he answers."
    beat "He takes his time, of which he has had a great deal."
  when Hobb is rattled:
    beat "He takes one hand off one ear to hear you, and puts it back."
end manner

In chapter 27 these two rows asked about the bell. They now ask about Hobb, which is what a manner should be about. The bell is the reason, and the reason is written once, somewhere else.

29.1.1 A mood of your own

rattled is not one of the thirteen. A story may define a mood by saying which platform mood it resembles.

define mood rattled like nervous, but darker

The line names the new mood and the platform mood it is like. It may end with , but and one of four words that shift it: restless, stiller, darker or brighter. A defined mood is used wherever a platform mood is: on a mood line, after change mood to, and in a condition.

29.1.2 Changing a mood

on every turn while the tower bell is tolling
  change mood to rattled
end on

change mood to has no person in it. It changes the mood of the person whose block or table the statement stands in. This clause stands in Hobb’s own block, so it is Hobb who is rattled. Written in one of Tamsin’s topic rows it would change Tamsin’s mood. Always write it in a person’s block or in one of that person’s tables, where it has someone to belong to.

> ask hobb about the bell
He takes one hand off one ear to hear you, and puts it back.

"It'll go on till it's told," Hobb shouts. "There's a word for the telling. I kept it by, in a newspaper, in case."

Hobb gives the hush page to Ansel.

The bell tolls again, and the rooks go up off the roof.

A mood does not stay changed. Left alone, a person settles back to the mood they began in over a few turns. That is why Hobb’s clause runs on every turn the bell tolls: it holds him rattled for as long as there is something to be rattled by. Hush the bell and he settles. For the first few turns afterwards his answers come with no sentence of manner at all, and then he is calm again and turning his kettle.

This is worth designing around. A mood is the right tool for how someone is taking the present moment. For a change that should last, use a feeling, or a state.

29.2 Feelings

A person may feel something toward another person, or toward any thing or place in the story.

feels wary of Ansel

Hobb’s block has that line, and so has Tamsin Vey’s, with a mood suspicious above it.

There are eight words, and they run from worst to best: despises, hates, dislikes, wary of, neutral, likes, trusts and devoted to. Two of them end in a preposition. After the other six, write toward before the name: feels trusts toward Ansel.

A feeling is read with the same words.

about "last night", "that night", "master":
  refuse when Hobb feels wary of Ansel: hobb-not-to-strangers
  phrase hobb-on-last-night when dimming was discussed

Hobb’s best story now has two locks on it. The player must have asked about the Dimming, as before, and Hobb must not be wary of the person asking.

> ask hobb about last night
Hobb looks at you for a while, narrowly, as a man looks at small print. "That's not a thing I tell to people I can't see," he says.

The refusal is the hint. Hobb has been looking for his spectacles since the spring, and chapter 24 made them something Ansel can mend.

29.2.1 Changing a feeling

on every turn while Hobb wears the spectacles, once
  change feeling toward Ansel to trusts
end on

This clause is in Hobb’s block too.

change feeling toward <someone> to <word> follows the rule of change mood to: the feeling changed is that of the person whose block the statement stands in. Unlike a mood, a feeling stays where it is put.

> give spectacles to hobb
You give the spectacles to Hobb.

Hobb hooks them over his ears and looks at you properly for the first time. "Ansel," he says, after a while. "You were always late."

> talk to hobb
He turns the kettle a quarter round on its hook, to no purpose.

"Still here," says Hobb, of one of you.

> ask hobb about last night
He looks at the blue flame on the sill before he answers.

Hobb is quiet for some time. "Somebody had to put the Lantern out," he says. "It was guttering. The Master said a dead light was worse than none. He didn't say who." He looks at his hands.

Tamsin’s feeling changes in the row that strikes the bargain.

define exchange the-price for Tamsin Vey
  answer "yes", "agreed", "done", "you have my word":
    phrase tamsin-shakes
    change Tamsin Vey to bound
    change feeling toward Ansel to likes
    Tamsin Vey gives the unbind page to Ansel

And one of her topic rows has two answers, chosen by that feeling.

about "magic", "craft":
  phrase tamsin-on-magic when Tamsin Vey feels wary of Ansel
  phrase tamsin-on-magic-warmer when Tamsin Vey feels likes toward Ansel
> ask tamsin about magic
"My mother kept a stall by this gate. The day it went out, so did the custom. Now you tell me it's coming back, and I am to be glad." She is not, particularly.
> ask tamsin about magic
"I'll tell you what I never told the old man. The night it went out, my mother's scales would not weigh true, and she laughed. She said it was the first honest day's trade she ever did." Tamsin Vey looks at the page in your hand. "Light your lamp. I would like to see if she was right."

The two phrases in that row carry conditions, and chapter 28 said that a row’s conditions must not overlap. Two different feelings toward the same person never do, and the compiler knows it.

A state would have done this job. Tamsin already has free and bound, and the second phrase could have asked whether she is bound. The feeling is better for two reasons. It says what you mean, so that the row still reads correctly when a second way of winning her round is added. And it is a scale and not a switch: a story can move a person from wary of to neutral to likes in three steps, and write a row for each.

29.3 What a person knows

knows the-last-night, witnessed, certain

A knows line in a person’s block says that the person holds a piece of knowledge when the story starts. The knowledge has a name of your choosing, a single hyphenated word, which stands for the thing known. The name does not need to be declared anywhere else.

Two more words follow it. The first is how the person came by it, and is required: witnessed, told, inferred, assumed or hallucinated. The second is how sure they are: uncertain, suspects, believes or certain.

A condition reads it with knows.

phrase hobb-keeps-it when Hobb knows the-last-night

By itself that is a flag, and a state would serve. The value of naming knowledge is that the names are shared. Two people can know the same thing, and a row in one person’s table can ask what another person knows.

29.3.1 Telling one another

Knowledge also moves. A spreads line says what a person passes on, and to whom.

create Nell
  a person, proper
  in the Yard
  mood calm
  knows theft, witnessed, certain
  spreads theft to anyone

  Nell.

create Tom
  a person, proper
  in the Yard
  mood calm
  spreads nothing

  Tom.

spreads is followed by the things spread, then to and an audience: anyone, trusted or allied. Several things are joined with and. When two people who each have a spreads line are in a room together, the platform has each tell the other what their line allows. Tom’s line is spreads nothing. He passes nothing on, and the line is what makes him someone who can be told.

> wait
Time passes...

You hear: "Nell mentions something to Tom."

From that turn Tom knows theft is true, and anything gated on it opens. The player, standing in the Yard, is told only that something was said. In another room the player would be told nothing, and Tom would know all the same.

That is rumour, in two lines for each person. A fact witnessed in the kitchen can reach the drawing room by way of whoever walks between them, and what the people there will say to the player changes as it arrives.

29.4 Build and try it

Copy relighting.story from the companion folder chapter-29 over your own file. It is the story with this chapter’s changes made, and it holds the phrases, which the chapter did not print in full. To make the changes by hand instead, work from this list and take the rest from that file. Add the define mood line at the top of the story. Give Hobb his feels and knows lines and the two on every turn clauses, and change his manner rows to read his mood. Give Tamsin Vey her mood and feels lines, add the change feeling line to her exchange, and split her row about magic in two.

sharpee compose relighting.story --check
sharpee play .

Ask Hobb about the Dimming and then about last night. Then find his spectacles, give them to him as they are, and ask again. Do this before you read the spark page. Hobb trusts whoever puts the spectacles on his nose, mended or not, and while the Hall is quiet he answers as the blocks above show. Once the bell has begun he is rattled, and he answers the same questions with one hand over an ear.

Under the Hood: the character model · Story IR character

Every line of this chapter is recorded under one key of the person’s entity in the compiled IR, character. It holds mood, a feels list with one entry for each person or thing felt about, and a knows list in which each entry carries its source and its confidence. The goals of chapter 26 are in the same place. The platform builds one model for each person from that key when the story loads, and it is the model’s own clock that brings a mood back to where it began.

From another system: feelings as relations

Neither manual describes a built-in model of mood or feeling. In Inform 10 the usual tool is a relation: a sentence declares that it “relates” people to people, another gives it a verb, and after that a sentence with the verb is a condition like any other. The TADS 3 documentation suggests a property on the actor, tested where a response should depend on the character’s frame of mind. Chord’s feels is a relation of the Inform kind that the language already knows about, with a fixed scale of eight values where Inform’s would be yours to define.

29.5 What you have so far

Hobb is rattled while the bell tolls and calm when it stops, and his manner follows his mood. He is wary of a stranger and trusts a returned student, and the difference is a pair of spectacles. Tamsin Vey warms to someone who has kept a bargain with her, and says so in her own way.
