6 Things

The rooms of Lanternhall describe a lamp, a kettle, a chair, a gate and a hearth, and the player can touch none of them. This chapter makes them. It also gives Ansel something to carry and puts the first other person in the story.

6.1 A thing and where it is

Anything in the world that is not a room, a door, a person, a container, a supporter or a region is a thing. A thing needs two facts: its kind, and its place.

create the kettle
  a thing
  in the Lodge

  A black iron kettle, still warm.

The line in the Lodge is the thing’s placement. It is the word in, an article, and the name of a room. The paragraph after the blank line is the thing’s description, which the player reads by examining it.

That is all a thing needs in order to be seen, examined, picked up, carried about and put down. You wrote none of those abilities. They come with being a thing.

You can see a kettle, a hand lamp, and Hobb here.

> examine lamp
A tin hand lamp with a wire handle. Its wick is dry and its glass is clean, which is Hobb all over.

> take lamp
Taken.

The line beginning “You can see” is the story’s own. After a room’s description it lists the things in the room that the description has not accounted for, and it chooses “a” or “an” for each one. The kettle and the hand lamp are listed because they are loose things a player might pick up.

A description is optional here too. A thing without one answers an examination with a stock line, which for the kettle would be “The kettle is just a kettle.”

6.2 Things that stay put

The Lodge’s description has already told the player about the chair and the newspapers. They should not be listed a second time, and nobody should walk off with the porter’s chair. Both problems have one answer, which is the word scenery.

create the chair
  a thing, scenery
  in the Lodge

  A wing chair that has taken the shape of one man.

scenery is the first of the trait words. A trait word is an adjective that follows the kind, after a comma, and changes what the thing is like. This one says two things about the chair: it is part of the room’s description, so the room does not list it, and it cannot be taken.

> take chair
The chair is fixed in place.

Scenery is how you keep the promises a room description makes. Read each of your descriptions as a player would, note every noun in it, and make a scenery thing for each one that a player might reasonably examine. The Relighting now does this for the blue flame on the sill, the chair, the newspapers, the gate, the hearth in the Refectory and the Great Lantern on the roof.

6.3 More than one, and things with names

Two more trait words are about grammar, the player’s and the story’s.

plural says that the thing’s name is a plural noun. The story then leaves out the “a” it would otherwise put in front of the name, so that a room lists “newspapers” and not “a newspapers”.

create the newspapers
  aka papers
  a thing, scenery, plural
  in the Lodge

  The top one is from last week. The bottom one reports the Dimming, in
  the small type newspapers use for things they do not yet believe.

proper says that the name is a proper name, one that takes no article. It is most often needed for people.

create Hobb
  a person, proper
  in the Lodge

  The porter of Lanternhall, who was old when you were a student and has
  not troubled to change since.

Without proper the Lodge would report that you can see “a Hobb” here. With it, he is listed as Hobb, and the story’s own messages use his name correctly:

> take hobb
You can't take Hobb.

Hobb is a person, not a thing, and people are placed the same way things are. He does nothing yet except stand in the Lodge and be examined. Part V is his.

6.4 Other words for a thing

The player will not always use your name for a thing. An aka line lists the other words they might use, separated by commas. It goes directly under the header, above the kind.

create the gate
  aka gates, arch, letters, motto
  a thing, scenery
  in the Lodge and the Gate Court

Now examine arch and examine motto both find the gate. Give a thing an aka for every noun your own prose uses about it. The Gate Court’s description speaks of “a line of cut letters”, so letters is on the list.

Be careful with the opposite problem. When two things in the same place can be meant by the same word, the story has to ask which one the player means, and it does so every time. If the Lodge held both a brass key and an iron key, then take key would be answered with a question. The cure is to choose names that do not share their last word, or to make sure the two things are never in the same place. This book called the lamp on the sill the blue flame for exactly that reason: there is a hand lamp in the same room, and the word lamp should mean only one of them.

6.5 A thing in several rooms

Look again at the gate’s placement.

  in the Lodge and the Gate Court

The gate can be seen from both rooms, so it is placed in both. A thing placed in more than one room is called a backdrop. With three or more rooms the list is written as English writes it, in the A, the B and the C.

A backdrop must be scenery. A thing the player could pick up cannot be in two places, and the compiler explains as much if you forget:

relighting.story:134:3 error [analysis.backdrop-not-scenery] `gate` is placed in several rooms — only scenery can be in more than one place (a thing the player could take cannot). Add `scenery`, or name one room.

Backdrops are for the sky, the river, the distant tower, and anything else that a player in several rooms would expect to be able to look at.

6.6 What Ansel carries

A person can begin the story holding things. A carries line, in the person’s own block, names one.

create the wax taper
  aka taper
  a thing

  Half a wax taper. Last week you lit it to read by, and its flame
  leaned toward your hand like a cat. You have carried it since.

create Ansel
  a person
  playable
  starts in the Lodge
  carries the wax taper

The taper has no placement line of its own. Ansel’s carries line places it. Write one carries line for each thing.

> inventory
You are carrying:

a wax taper

A thing that has no placement at all, and that nobody carries, is not an error. It exists and is nowhere. That is useful later, for things that enter the story part of the way through, and chapter 15 shows how to bring one on.

6.7 Hidden things

One more trait word belongs here. A thing that is concealed is in its room but cannot be seen, examined or taken until the player finds it by searching.

create the spectacles
  aka glasses
  a thing, plural, concealed
  in the Lodge

  Wire spectacles with one arm mended in thread. Hobb has been looking
  for them since the spring.
> examine spectacles
You can't see any such thing.

> search
Hidden here, you discover: spectacles.

> take glasses
Taken.

Once found, a concealed thing is an ordinary thing. Use this sparingly as well. A player has no reason to type search unless your prose gives them one, and thirty years of newspapers is the Lodge’s hint.

The companion word is hiding-spot, which marks a thing the player can hide behind. The Relighting has no use for one, so here is a curtain from another story:

create the curtain
  a thing, scenery, hiding-spot
  in the Den

  A heavy curtain.
> hide behind curtain
You slip behind the curtain.

6.8 Build and try it

Copy relighting.story from the companion folder chapter-06 over your own file. It is the story with this chapter’s changes made, and it holds the things and descriptions the chapter did not print in full, the hand lamp, the blue flame, the hearth and the Great Lantern among them. Check it and play it.

sharpee compose relighting.story --check
sharpee play .

Take the hand lamp, carry it north, and put it down in the Gate Court.

> drop lamp
Dropped.

> look
Gate Court
A square of worn flagstones between the gate and the cloister arch. Grass has come up through the joints and been left to get on with it. Over the gate, a line of cut letters says that the craft needs a light to come home to.

You can see a hand lamp here.

Then examine everything a description mentions, in every room. Where the answer is “You can’t see any such thing”, you have found a promise the story is not yet keeping. Some of those are deliberate, because the Library’s desks and the bell rope have chapters of their own coming. Others stay unkept in The Relighting, which makes scenery only of what the game uses. Nothing in this step needs fixing. In a story of your own, each of them is a line to write.

Under the Hood: traits · sharpee compose

A kind and its trait words become traits on the platform, which are the units of ability it builds everything from. scenery is the scenery trait, and the standard taking action refuses anything that has it, which is where “fixed in place” comes from. a person brings the actor trait, and taking refuses that as well, with a different sentence. The words you write are a closed list. You cannot invent a trait word by typing one, and a word the compiler does not know is reported as analysis.trait-not-declared. Chapter 24 shows how to declare traits of your own.

From another system: scenery and backdrops

Both ideas will be familiar. Inform 10 has scenery as a property of a thing and a backdrop as a kind of its own, placed in several rooms or in a region. TADS 3 has a class for a thing that must exist in more than one place at a time. In Chord, scenery is a trait word, and a backdrop is not a kind at all. It is simply scenery whose placement names more than one room. The other difference is that Chord does not let a thing be portable by declaration and immovable by rule: scenery is the one switch, and it covers both the listing and the taking.

6.9 What you have so far

The Lodge has a porter, his chair, his papers, his kettle and two lamps. Ansel is carrying a taper. The gate can be seen from both sides, and the hearth and the Great Lantern can be examined. Nothing yet can be opened, and nothing can be put on or in anything else. That is the next chapter.
