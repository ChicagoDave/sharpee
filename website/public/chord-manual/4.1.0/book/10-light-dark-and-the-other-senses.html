10 Light, dark and the other senses

The Relighting is a story about a light that has gone out, and so far every room in it is as bright as every other. This chapter makes one room dark, makes the hand lamp a lamp, and then looks briefly at what the player can smell and hear.

10.1 A dark room

A room is lit unless you say otherwise. The word that says otherwise is dark, and it goes on the room’s header line after the kind.

create the Undercroft, a room, dark

  Low vaults on squat pillars, running off under the whole of the hall.
  The air is cold and smells of stone. The steps go back up.

dark is a trait word for rooms only. In a dark room the player cannot see. The room’s description is not shown, the things in it are not listed, and nothing there can be examined or taken, because as far as the player can tell nothing is there.

> down
It is pitch dark. You are likely to be eaten by a grue.

> look
It's pitch dark, and you can't see a thing.

> examine crate
You can't see any such thing.

> up
Cloister
A covered walk around a garth gone to nettles. The refectory is east, the library door west, and the foot of the bell stair north. Worn steps go down into the dark under the hall.

Notice three things. The player was allowed to walk into the dark, and was allowed to walk out of it again. The exits of a dark room still work, so a player who blunders in is never trapped. And the first line the player read came from another story entirely.

The grue is the platform’s stock line for entering a dark room, kept in honour of the games that invented this kind of puzzle. It has no place in Lanternhall, where the only thing in the dark is a crate. Chapter 22 shows how to replace any of the platform’s messages with your own, and this is the first one The Relighting replaces. Until then, leave it. Knowing which lines in your story are not yours is worth a little embarrassment.

10.2 A light to carry

A thing gives light if it is a light-source, and you say how it begins.

create the hand lamp
  a thing, light-source, starts unlit
  in the Lodge

  A tin hand lamp with a wire handle. Its wick is dry and its glass is
  clean, which is Hobb all over.

starts lit and starts unlit are the two starting states, and like every starting state they need their trait word. A thing that is not a light-source cannot start lit:

relighting.story:259:12 error [analysis.starts-state-pairing] `starts lit` requires `light-source` composed on this entity.

A light source that is lit lights the room it is in. It does so whether the player is holding it or has put it down, and it goes on doing so if the player leaves and comes back.

> light lamp
You light the hand lamp.
> down
Undercroft
Low vaults on squat pillars, running off under the whole of the hall. The air is cold and smells of stone. The steps go back up.

You can see a crate here.

The player lights and puts out a light source with the ordinary verbs, and the platform notices when doing so changes what can be seen.

> extinguish lamp
You put out the hand lamp, and darkness closes in.

> look
It's pitch dark, and you can't see a thing.

> light lamp
You light the hand lamp, and the darkness draws back.

Undercroft
Low vaults on squat pillars, running off under the whole of the hall. The air is cold and smells of stone. The steps go back up.

You can see a crate here.

Only a light source can be lit. Ansel’s taper is a plain thing, and the story says so:

> light taper
The wax taper isn't something you can light.

10.3 What this does to the story

With two words added, The Relighting has changed shape. The lens is in a crate in the Undercroft, the Undercroft is dark, and the lamp is in the Lodge. A player who goes straight down the steps learns that they need a light, and has to go back for one. The world now has an order in which things must be done, and you wrote no rule to enforce it. The darkness enforces it.

There is one thing wrong, and it is worth seeing clearly. The lamp lights when the player types light lamp. In the finished story, lighting that lamp is the first act of magic anyone has performed in thirty years, and it should not be available for the asking. For now it is. The standard action that lights a light source does not know that this one is special. Teaching it takes a requirement from chapter 15 and a new verb from chapter 25, and until then the lamp is merely a lamp.

10.4 Seeing in the dark

A person may be given night-vision, which lets them see in a dark room as though it were lit. It is a trait word for people only.

create Ansel
  a person, proper, night-vision
  playable
  starts in the Cellar

For a person who is the player, the dark simply stops mattering. Ansel has no such gift. The word is more often useful on someone else, in a story where a character must find their way about a dark house on their own turns, as Part V describes.

10.5 Smell and sound

The player has other senses, and two trait words speak to them.

A thing that is smelly can be smelled from anywhere in its room, and a thing that is loud can be heard. What makes these two worth having is that they pass through a closed container. Here is a tin with three things in it, from another story.

create the tin
  a container, openable, starts closed
  in the Den

  A tin.

create the cheese
  a thing, smelly
  in the tin

  A ripe cheese.

create the clock
  a thing, loud
  in the tin

  A clock.

create the sock
  a thing
  in the tin

  A sock.

With the tin shut, the player can see none of the three, and can detect two.

> smell
You smell a cheese.

> listen
You hear a clock.

> listen to tin
You hear faint sounds from inside the tin.

This is a way of telling the player that a closed thing is worth opening without saying so. The sock, which is neither smelly nor loud, gives no sign of itself until the tin is open.

Sound goes much further than this. A story can define sounds of its own, play them, and have people in the story react to hearing them, and The Relighting does all three with its tower bell. Those need clauses from Part III and are taught in chapter 36.

10.6 Build and try it

Copy relighting.story from the companion folder chapter-10 over your own file. It is the story with this chapter’s changes made. To make the changes by hand instead, work from this list and take the rest from that file: add dark to the Undercroft, make the hand lamp a light source, and let the Cloister’s description warn of the dark at the bottom of the steps.

sharpee compose relighting.story --check
sharpee play .

Walk down without the lamp, then fetch it, light it and walk down with it. Then try leaving the lit lamp in the Undercroft and coming back.

> drop lamp
Dropped.

> up
Cloister
A covered walk around a garth gone to nettles. The refectory is east, the library door west, and the foot of the bell stair north. Worn steps go down into the dark under the hall.

> down
Undercroft
Low vaults on squat pillars, running off under the whole of the hall. The air is cold and smells of stone. The steps go back up.

You can see a crate and a hand lamp here.

Under the Hood: perception · if.event.perception.blocked

Darkness is not handled by the actions. The looking action describes the Undercroft in exactly the same way whether or not there is light. Between the action and the player sits a perception step, which asks whether the player can see where they are. If they cannot, it replaces what the action reported with a single event saying that sight was blocked, and “It’s pitch dark, and you can’t see a thing” is how that event is worded. This is why you did not have to write a dark version of the room. There is only one version, and the platform decides whether the player gets it.

From another system: light

Both systems have this model, and it will feel like home. Inform 10 has dark rooms and lit things, and TADS 3 has a brightness on rooms and on light sources. Chord’s version is the simple one: a room is dark or it is not, a light source is lit or it is not, and one lit source in the room is enough. There are no degrees of brightness. The surprise for both kinds of author is further on, in that lighting a light source is an ordinary standard action that you restrict, not a rule you write from nothing.

10.7 What you have so far

Lanternhall has a dark place under it and one lamp to see by, and the story has its first forced order of events. The next chapter steps back from single rooms and things to look at the map as a whole.
